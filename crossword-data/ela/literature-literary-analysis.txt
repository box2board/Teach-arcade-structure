Exposition|Opening information that introduces a story's characters and situation.
Inciting Incident|Event that disrupts routine and starts the central problem.
Rising Action|Plot phase in which obstacles increase tension before the decisive moment.
Climax|Decisive high point when the central struggle reaches its greatest intensity.
Falling Action|Events showing consequences after the decisive high point.
Resolution|Plot phase in which the central conflict is settled.
Flashback|Scene presenting events from an earlier time.
Foreshadowing|Hints that prepare readers for later events.
Subplot|Smaller storyline alongside the central plot.
Pacing|Speed at which a story unfolds for the reader.
In Medias Res|Latin term for beginning in the middle of the action.
Frame Narrative|Structure in which one story surrounds another story.
Episodic|Describes a structure built from relatively self-contained incidents.
Linear|Describes a narrative presenting events in chronological order.
Nonlinear|Describes a narrative moving outside chronological order.
Denouement|Closing aftermath that shows life after the central conflict has been settled.
Cliffhanger|Ending that leaves the result of an urgent event unknown.
Parallel Plots|Separate storylines that develop alongside each other and may intersect.
Chronology|Order in which events occur in time.
Turning Point|Moment that changes the direction of the story.

Indirect|Describes characterization revealed through actions, speech, and thoughts.
Protagonist|Central character pursuing the story's main goal.
Antagonist|Character or force opposing the central character's goal.
Static|Describes a character who remains largely unchanged.
Dynamic|Describes a character whose beliefs or behavior significantly change.
Foil|Character whose contrasting qualities highlight another character's traits.
Confidant|Trusted person to whom a character reveals private concerns.
Motivation|Reason behind a character's decisions or actions.
Trait|Characteristic such as patience or generosity.
Dialogue|Characters' spoken exchanges that can reveal personality.
Actions|Things characters do that provide evidence of their qualities.
Thoughts|Private mental responses that can differ from confident spoken words.
Development|Growth or change in a character across the story.
Accountability|Willingness to accept responsibility rather than blame others.
Resourcefulness|Ability to solve problems creatively using available materials.
Trust|Confidence in another person that can grow through shared experience.
Inference|Conclusion about a character drawn from clues and reasoning.
Contrast|Difference between characters' qualities that helps readers compare them.

Interpretation|Reasoned explanation of what a text means.
Contrast|Difference between characters' choices and outcomes that can reveal a theme.
Curiosity|Desire to ask questions and learn.
Perseverance|Continued effort despite setbacks.
Priorities|Things a character considers most important.
Dialogue|Exchange of views that can develop a message about listening.
Claim|Arguable statement about a text's meaning that requires support.

Perspective|Position from which events are seen and understood.
Pronouns|Words such as I, you, and she that help signal viewpoint.
Reliability|Degree to which a narrator's account can be trusted.

Author|Real person who writes the text, distinct from its created teller.

Suspense|Uncertainty about what will happen, sometimes created by restricted information.
Voice|Distinctive style or personality expressed by a storyteller.
Selective|Describes narrative access that includes some information and leaves out other information.
Shifting|Describes a viewpoint that moves between different characters or narrators.
Unreliable|Describes a teller whose account gives readers reasons for doubt.
Knowledge|Information available to a narrator or character.
Account|Version of events that can differ when two people describe the same incident.

External|Describes a struggle against a force outside the character.

Character Versus Technology|Conflict involving a person and a machine or automated system.

Antagonist|Person or force blocking the central character's goal.
Goal|Result a character tries to achieve.
Obstacle|Barrier preventing progress toward a desired result.
Tension|Pressure created by unresolved struggles.
Dilemma|Choice between competing options that each present difficulties.
Resolution|Settlement of the central struggle.
Guilt|Feeling arising when a character believes they have done wrong.
Values|Principles a character weighs when making a moral choice.
Cooperation|Working together, sometimes replacing rivalry under a shared threat.
Setting|Time and place whose conditions can influence a struggle.
Forgiveness|Release of resentment that may settle a relationship struggle.
