[ela--reading-informational-text--main-idea-and-supporting-details]
Main Idea|Central point that a paragraph's details explain or support.
Supporting Details|Facts and examples developing a paragraph's central point.
Summary|Short account preserving the most important information.
Topic|General subject a passage discusses.
Paragraph|Group of sentences developing a connected point.
Examples|Specific instances used to illustrate a larger statement.
Facts|Statements that can be checked against evidence.
Heading|Title identifying the subject of a section.
Synthesize|Combine related information into a larger understanding.
Relevant|Describes a detail connected to the central point.
Statistic|Numerical information that may support a broader claim.
Claim|Position or assertion a writer develops with support.
Explicit|Describes an idea stated directly in the passage.
Implicit|Describes an idea suggested rather than directly stated.
Concise|Brief while still expressing the necessary meaning.
Qualification|Detail that limits or adds conditions to a broad statement.
Contradiction|Information that conflicts with a claim and requires closer examination.
Significance|Importance of a detail beyond simply reporting it.
Repeated|Describes information returning across sections and helping signal central importance.
Minor Detail|Less important information usually omitted from a short account.
[ela--reading-informational-text--inferences-and-textual-evidence]
Inference|Conclusion combining clues with reasoning or prior knowledge.
Textual Evidence|Information from the passage supporting a conclusion.
Prior Knowledge|Relevant understanding a reader brings from previous experience.
Reasoning|Thinking that connects available facts to a conclusion.
Claim|Assertion about a text that needs support.
Relevant|Describes evidence directly connected to the conclusion being tested.
Explicit|Describes information directly stated by the text.
Implicit|Describes information suggested without being directly stated.
Observation|Detail a reader can identify before deciding what it means.
Support|Information that strengthens an interpretation rather than undermining it.
Context|Surrounding information helping readers understand a clue.
Consistency|Agreement between an interpretation and the text's clear statements.
Exception|Case that differs from a general pattern and must still be considered.
Revise|Change a conclusion when evidence does not fit it.
Cautious|Describes a conclusion that acknowledges uncertainty instead of claiming too much.
Correlation|Association between changes that does not by itself prove cause.
Causation|Relationship in which one event produces or contributes to another.
Pattern|Repeated actions or details that strengthen an interpretation.
Contradiction|Conflict between an inference and a clear textual statement.
Quotation|Exact source words a reader can cite as support.
[ela--reading-informational-text--informational-text-structures]
Text Structure|Way a writer organizes information and ideas.
Problem and Solution|Organization presenting a difficulty and possible responses.
Compare and Contrast|Organization examining similarities and differences.
Cause and Effect|Organization explaining why something happened and its results.
Sequence|Ordered series of steps or stages.
Description|Organization presenting a subject's parts, qualities, or features.
Chronological|Describes order from earlier events to later events.
Signal Words|Terms such as however or therefore that suggest relationships.
Process|Series of actions explaining how something is done.
Definition|Explanation of what a term means.
Cause|Event or condition producing a result.
Effect|Result produced by an event or condition.
Similarity|Feature two subjects share.
Difference|Feature distinguishing one subject from another.
Heading|Section title that can indicate how ideas are organized.
Transition|Word or phrase connecting ideas or sections.
Causation|Relationship a reader must distinguish from mere time order.
Mixed Structure|Organization combining two or more patterns in one text.
Components|Parts listed when explaining the features of a subject.
Summary|Brief account that should preserve the central organizational relationships.
