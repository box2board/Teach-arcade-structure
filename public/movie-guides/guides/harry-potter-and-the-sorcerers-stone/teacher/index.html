Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How do Harry’s choices reveal the person he is becoming?

  • Explain how belonging shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce inference, characterization, setting, and adaptation. Use the 2001 first film, released as Philosopher’s Stone in some countries. Provide a selected novel passage for comparison.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Bullying, emotional mistreatment, and references to murdered parents.
  • Frightening creatures, dangerous magic, injury, and intense confrontation.
  • Themes of loss and some mild language; preview for younger students.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the Sorting, the troll encounter, the Mirror of Erised, and the final confrontation.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

Magic belongs to the fictional world. Distinguish a viewer’s inference from confirmed information: the film deliberately directs suspicion toward Snape before revealing Quirrell. Compare what the film retains and omits from the novel.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How do Harry’s choices reveal the person he is becoming?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a story show belonging through actions rather than labels?
    Characters can include, defend, or listen to someone; membership alone does not demonstrate care.
  2. How does the Dursleys’ treatment establish Harry’s situation?
    Their exclusion and restrictions show that he lacks support and recognition at home.
  3. What changes when Hagrid explains Harry’s background?
    Harry learns about his parents and a community he did not know existed.
  4. How does Diagon Alley introduce the fantasy setting?
    Ordinary needs such as supplies are transformed through magical shops, objects, and customs.
  5. How does the Sorting show that choice matters to Harry?
    He expresses a preference rather than passively accepting how others classify him.
  6. How does the troll encounter change the trio’s relationship?
    Their cooperation and Hermione’s response help turn tension into friendship.
  7. Why is Hermione’s research important to the mystery?
    Her knowledge helps connect Nicolas Flamel with the guarded Stone.
  8. What caution does Dumbledore offer about the mirror?
    Dwelling on desire can distract someone from living and making choices in the present.
  9. How do the obstacles require different strengths?
    The group relies on knowledge, courage, and strategy rather than one person doing everything.
  10. Why can Harry obtain the Stone from the mirror?
    He wants to find it without using its power, distinguishing his motive from Quirrell’s.
  11. What choice best demonstrates Harry’s developing values?
    Accept a supported example such as choosing friends, protecting others, or resisting the Stone’s misuse.
  12. How does film form emphasize one moment of belonging?
    Identify framing, music, dialogue, or performance in a specific scene and explain its effect.
  13. What changes in a selected novel passage’s film adaptation?
    Identify a specific omission or alteration and explain its effect without assuming every change is a mistake.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a story show belonging through actions rather than labels?
    Characters can include, defend, or listen to someone; membership alone does not demonstrate care.
  2. What makes an inference stronger than a guess?
    It connects observable clues to an explanation while remaining open to correction.
  3. How does the Dursleys’ treatment establish Harry’s situation?
    Their exclusion and restrictions show that he lacks support and recognition at home.
  4. What changes when Hagrid explains Harry’s background?
    Harry learns about his parents and a community he did not know existed.
  5. How does Diagon Alley introduce the fantasy setting?
    Ordinary needs such as supplies are transformed through magical shops, objects, and customs.
  6. What does Harry’s encounter with Draco suggest about friendship?
    Harry resists status-based contempt rather than accepting an alliance that demeans others.
  7. How does the Sorting show that choice matters to Harry?
    He expresses a preference rather than passively accepting how others classify him.
  8. How does the troll encounter change the trio’s relationship?
    Their cooperation and Hermione’s response help turn tension into friendship.
  9. Why is Hermione’s research important to the mystery?
    Her knowledge helps connect Nicolas Flamel with the guarded Stone.
  10. What does Harry see in the Mirror of Erised?
    He sees his parents and family, revealing a deep desire for connection.
  11. What caution does Dumbledore offer about the mirror?
    Dwelling on desire can distract someone from living and making choices in the present.
  12. Why do the children suspect Snape?
    His behavior and apparent connection to danger seem to fit their interpretation, though the clues are incomplete.
  13. How do the obstacles require different strengths?
    The group relies on knowledge, courage, and strategy rather than one person doing everything.
  14. What does Ron’s chess decision contribute?
    He accepts personal risk so the others can continue toward their goal.
  15. How does the final revelation change earlier suspicions?
    Quirrell’s role shows that the trio misread some clues and Snape’s actions.
  16. Why can Harry obtain the Stone from the mirror?
    He wants to find it without using its power, distinguishing his motive from Quirrell’s.
  17. What choice best demonstrates Harry’s developing values?
    Accept a supported example such as choosing friends, protecting others, or resisting the Stone’s misuse.
  18. How does the House Cup ending connect to earlier actions?
    Recognition is tied to acts of courage, loyalty, and standing up to friends.
  19. How does film form emphasize one moment of belonging?
    Identify framing, music, dialogue, or performance in a specific scene and explain its effect.
  20. What changes in a selected novel passage’s film adaptation?
    Identify a specific omission or alteration and explain its effect without assuming every change is a mistake.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
