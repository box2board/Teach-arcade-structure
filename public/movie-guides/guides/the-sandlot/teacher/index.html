Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does the group turn an unfamiliar newcomer into a participant?

  • Explain how belonging shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce narrator, nostalgia, rumor, and mentorship. Preview sexist insults, chewing-tobacco sickness, and the pool scene’s deceptive unwanted kiss. Do not imitate these behaviors as a class activity.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Sexist and derogatory insults, bullying, and some profanity.
  • A boy deceives a lifeguard to obtain an unwanted kiss; tobacco use causes vomiting.
  • Dog pursuit, unsafe stunts, and a frightening fence collapse.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after Benny teaches Smalls, after the borrowed ball is lost, after failed recovery plans, and after Mr. Mertle’s account.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

Adult narration idealizes a selected childhood summer. Analyze kindness and exclusion together rather than endorsing every joke. The dog’s reputation is a useful example of how rumor can distort evidence.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does the group turn an unfamiliar newcomer into a participant?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes mentorship different from showing off a skill?
    A mentor helps another person participate and improve rather than only displaying superiority.
  2. Why does Smalls struggle to join the group?
    He is new and lacks the baseball knowledge and confidence others already share.
  3. How does Benny help him participate?
    He offers practical instruction and creates a chance for Smalls to contribute.
  4. What do the group’s jokes reveal about both belonging and exclusion?
    Shared humor can build connection while some insults and behaviors harm others.
  5. Why should the pool scene be questioned despite its comic framing?
    Deception does not make an unwanted kiss consensual or acceptable.
  6. What does the chewing-tobacco episode show about imitation?
    Copying admired adult behavior can have harmful consequences.
  7. Why does Smalls bring a ball from home?
    He wants to keep the game going without understanding the object’s significance.
  8. How does the legend of the Beast affect their plans?
    Fear based on secondhand stories makes an ordinary retrieval seem impossible.
  9. How does Benny’s imagined encounter with Babe Ruth affect his choice?
    The fantasy encourages a bold attempt within the film’s story about courage and legacy.
  10. What does Mr. Mertle’s account add?
    His baseball history and offer reveal knowledge hidden behind the group’s assumptions.
  11. Which action most clearly supports a newcomer’s belonging?
    Describe practical instruction or inclusion rather than acceptance based only on talent.
  12. What does the film teach about testing a rumor?
    Seek direct and appropriate evidence before treating a dramatic story as established fact.
  13. What limitation should accompany its portrait of childhood?
    The selected nostalgic account does not represent every child’s experience or justify its harmful jokes.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes mentorship different from showing off a skill?
    A mentor helps another person participate and improve rather than only displaying superiority.
  2. How can nostalgia influence a narrator’s account?
    It can emphasize treasured moments and soften or overlook troubling details.
  3. Why does Smalls struggle to join the group?
    He is new and lacks the baseball knowledge and confidence others already share.
  4. How does Benny help him participate?
    He offers practical instruction and creates a chance for Smalls to contribute.
  5. What do the group’s jokes reveal about both belonging and exclusion?
    Shared humor can build connection while some insults and behaviors harm others.
  6. How does the baseball lot function as a setting?
    It provides an informal space for play, relationships, and group routines.
  7. Why should the pool scene be questioned despite its comic framing?
    Deception does not make an unwanted kiss consensual or acceptable.
  8. What does the chewing-tobacco episode show about imitation?
    Copying admired adult behavior can have harmful consequences.
  9. Why does Smalls bring a ball from home?
    He wants to keep the game going without understanding the object’s significance.
  10. Why is losing that ball different from losing an ordinary baseball?
    It carries a valuable signature and belongs to his stepfather.
  11. How does the legend of the Beast affect their plans?
    Fear based on secondhand stories makes an ordinary retrieval seem impossible.
  12. Why do the mechanical retrieval attempts fail?
    Their assumptions and escalating designs do not provide a workable solution.
  13. How does Benny’s imagined encounter with Babe Ruth affect his choice?
    The fantasy encourages a bold attempt within the film’s story about courage and legacy.
  14. What danger follows his retrieval attempt?
    The dog pursues him, and the chase ends with a fence collapse.
  15. How does meeting the dog change the group’s understanding?
    Direct experience challenges the frightening legend they had accepted.
  16. What does Mr. Mertle’s account add?
    His baseball history and offer reveal knowledge hidden behind the group’s assumptions.
  17. Which action most clearly supports a newcomer’s belonging?
    Describe practical instruction or inclusion rather than acceptance based only on talent.
  18. How does the narrator’s adult perspective shape one scene?
    Explain a specific emphasis, commentary, or nostalgic presentation.
  19. What does the film teach about testing a rumor?
    Seek direct and appropriate evidence before treating a dramatic story as established fact.
  20. What limitation should accompany its portrait of childhood?
    The selected nostalgic account does not represent every child’s experience or justify its harmful jokes.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
