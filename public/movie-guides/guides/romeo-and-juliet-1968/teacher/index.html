Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How do private choices and public conflict combine to produce tragedy?

  • Explain how family conflict shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Identify this as Zeffirelli’s 1968 film, distinct from the 1996 Romeo + Juliet guide. Introduce feud, prologue, dramatic irony, banishment, and soliloquy. Provide a teacher-selected play passage; preview the bedroom scene before deciding what to show.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Brief bedroom nudity and implied sexual activity involving teenage characters.
  • Sword fights, bloody injury, deaths, and suicide by poison and stabbing.
  • Threatening family pressure and sexual jokes; emotional intensity.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the ball, after the balcony conversation, following the street deaths, and after the tomb sequence.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The film cuts and rearranges Shakespeare’s play. Its Renaissance design is an interpretation rather than proof of the play’s exact historical world. Compare the tomb sequence with Act 5, scene 3: notably, the film omits Romeo’s encounter with Paris there. Do not romanticize the deaths or reduce the outcome to a single cause.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How do private choices and public conflict combine to produce tragedy?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a setting influence the way an audience hears dialogue?
    Architecture, costumes, and social spaces frame relationships and expectations around the words.
  2. What does the opening street conflict establish?
    The family feud affects public life and involves people beyond the lovers.
  3. How does Romeo behave before meeting Juliet?
    He is absorbed in an earlier romantic disappointment involving Rosaline.
  4. How do performance and music frame the meeting at the ball?
    Attention, movement, and the song create intimacy within a crowded family celebration.
  5. How does the balcony setting help express the relationship’s obstacles?
    Physical separation and the risk of being discovered make their privacy fragile.
  6. Why does Friar Laurence support the marriage?
    He hopes the union may help reconcile the families, though secrecy introduces risk.
  7. Why does Romeo initially refuse Tybalt’s challenge?
    His secret marriage creates a family connection Tybalt does not know about.
  8. What follows Romeo’s killing of Tybalt?
    He is banished, separating the lovers and increasing urgency.
  9. What pressure does Juliet face from her father?
    He demands that she marry Paris and responds angrily to her refusal.
  10. What does the final procession communicate about the feud?
    The families confront the shared consequences of their hostility after the lovers die.
  11. Choose a scene in which the audience knows more than a character.
    Accept the secret marriage, potion plan, or tomb events with accurate explanation.
  12. What difference should you examine between the film’s tomb scene and the play?
    The omitted encounter with Paris is one example; explain how a cut changes emphasis.
  13. Which adults have opportunities to reduce the conflict?
    Accept supported analysis of parents, the Friar, the Nurse, or the Prince without assigning one simple cause.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a setting influence the way an audience hears dialogue?
    Architecture, costumes, and social spaces frame relationships and expectations around the words.
  2. What makes dramatic irony different from ordinary surprise?
    The audience knows information a character lacks, changing the meaning of a decision.
  3. What does the opening street conflict establish?
    The family feud affects public life and involves people beyond the lovers.
  4. How does Romeo behave before meeting Juliet?
    He is absorbed in an earlier romantic disappointment involving Rosaline.
  5. How do performance and music frame the meeting at the ball?
    Attention, movement, and the song create intimacy within a crowded family celebration.
  6. Why does learning each other’s family name matter?
    They recognize that their attraction conflicts with inherited loyalties and hostility.
  7. How does the balcony setting help express the relationship’s obstacles?
    Physical separation and the risk of being discovered make their privacy fragile.
  8. Why does Friar Laurence support the marriage?
    He hopes the union may help reconcile the families, though secrecy introduces risk.
  9. Why does Romeo initially refuse Tybalt’s challenge?
    His secret marriage creates a family connection Tybalt does not know about.
  10. How does Mercutio’s death change Romeo’s choices?
    Grief and anger push him toward retaliation against Tybalt.
  11. What follows Romeo’s killing of Tybalt?
    He is banished, separating the lovers and increasing urgency.
  12. How does the farewell scene contrast intimacy with danger?
    The lovers share a private moment while departure and discovery threaten their future.
  13. What pressure does Juliet face from her father?
    He demands that she marry Paris and responds angrily to her refusal.
  14. What is Friar Laurence’s plan for Juliet?
    A potion will make her appear dead until she can reunite with Romeo.
  15. Why does Romeo misunderstand what has happened?
    The intended explanation does not reach him, and he receives news of apparent death.
  16. What does the final procession communicate about the feud?
    The families confront the shared consequences of their hostility after the lovers die.
  17. Choose a scene in which the audience knows more than a character.
    Accept the secret marriage, potion plan, or tomb events with accurate explanation.
  18. How does the use of period costumes affect your interpretation?
    Accept a supported claim about family status, social rules, or distance from modern life.
  19. What difference should you examine between the film’s tomb scene and the play?
    The omitted encounter with Paris is one example; explain how a cut changes emphasis.
  20. Which adults have opportunities to reduce the conflict?
    Accept supported analysis of parents, the Friar, the Nurse, or the Prince without assigning one simple cause.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
