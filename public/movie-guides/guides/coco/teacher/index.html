Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does discovering a fuller family story change Miguel’s choices?

  • Explain how family memory shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce remembrance, family narrative, evidence, and musical motif. Explain that Miguel is the young protagonist and Coco is his great-grandmother. Discuss the film’s fantasy separately from varied real Día de Muertos practices; no required family-loss disclosures.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Skeleton imagery, death, a murder revelation, and threats of being forgotten.
  • Grief, separation, and distress involving an elderly family member.
  • Peril and some intense confrontations despite the colorful fantasy setting.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the music ban is explained, after meeting Héctor, after the family revelation, and before the final song with Coco.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

Use a teacher-selected cultural source to contextualize the tradition without treating one fictional family as representative of all Mexican people. The same song has different meanings depending on its performer, audience, and relationship.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Disney film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does discovering a fuller family story change Miguel’s choices?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a family story change when new evidence appears?
    An omitted perspective or detail can alter the meaning of an inherited account.
  2. Why does Miguel’s family reject music?
    An inherited account of abandonment connects music with harm to the family.
  3. Why does Miguel admire Ernesto de la Cruz?
    Public fame and performances offer a model for the musical future he wants.
  4. How does the damaged family photograph influence his assumption?
    An incomplete image leads him to connect the missing ancestor with the famous musician.
  5. Why does Imelda’s condition on her blessing matter?
    Miguel must negotiate between returning home and continuing music.
  6. How does Héctor’s need differ from Miguel’s goal?
    Héctor wants to reach the living world before remembrance of him disappears.
  7. What does the forgotten musician’s disappearance reveal?
    The fantasy gives memory a concrete role in sustaining the dead.
  8. Why does Ernesto’s public image become unreliable evidence?
    Celebrity performance conceals harmful actions and appropriation of another person’s work.
  9. How does the murder revelation alter the apparent abandonment?
    It shows that a forced separation was mistaken for a voluntary rejection.
  10. What changes in the family’s later remembrance?
    Héctor’s place and music are restored within a fuller account of the past.
  11. Which object functions as evidence, and what are its limits?
    Analyze the photograph, guitar, or letters without assuming an object explains everything alone.
  12. What cultural question should be checked beyond the fantasy?
    Select a question about Día de Muertos and use a reliable cultural source.
  13. How does the ending connect artistic ambition with responsibility?
    Miguel can pursue music while recognizing family relationships and the need for truthful remembrance.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a family story change when new evidence appears?
    An omitted perspective or detail can alter the meaning of an inherited account.
  2. Why can the same song mean different things to different listeners?
    Relationships and circumstances affect how its words and performance are understood.
  3. Why does Miguel’s family reject music?
    An inherited account of abandonment connects music with harm to the family.
  4. Why does Miguel admire Ernesto de la Cruz?
    Public fame and performances offer a model for the musical future he wants.
  5. How does the damaged family photograph influence his assumption?
    An incomplete image leads him to connect the missing ancestor with the famous musician.
  6. What changes after he takes the guitar?
    The fantasy places him in the Land of the Dead, creating a new problem and search.
  7. Why does Imelda’s condition on her blessing matter?
    Miguel must negotiate between returning home and continuing music.
  8. How does Héctor’s need differ from Miguel’s goal?
    Héctor wants to reach the living world before remembrance of him disappears.
  9. What does the forgotten musician’s disappearance reveal?
    The fantasy gives memory a concrete role in sustaining the dead.
  10. How do Miguel and Héctor help one another initially?
    They exchange skills and access while pursuing different immediate needs.
  11. Why does Ernesto’s public image become unreliable evidence?
    Celebrity performance conceals harmful actions and appropriation of another person’s work.
  12. What does Miguel learn about Héctor’s family connection?
    Héctor is Coco’s father, changing the meaning of the family’s story.
  13. How does the murder revelation alter the apparent abandonment?
    It shows that a forced separation was mistaken for a voluntary rejection.
  14. Why does returning become urgent?
    Héctor’s survival in the fantasy depends on Coco’s remaining memory.
  15. Why does Miguel sing to Coco rather than merely present an argument?
    The personal song offers a connection to her remembered relationship with her father.
  16. What changes in the family’s later remembrance?
    Héctor’s place and music are restored within a fuller account of the past.
  17. Which object functions as evidence, and what are its limits?
    Analyze the photograph, guitar, or letters without assuming an object explains everything alone.
  18. How does one song’s changing use develop the theme?
    Compare two performances and explain how context changes meaning.
  19. What cultural question should be checked beyond the fantasy?
    Select a question about Día de Muertos and use a reliable cultural source.
  20. How does the ending connect artistic ambition with responsibility?
    Miguel can pursue music while recognizing family relationships and the need for truthful remembrance.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
