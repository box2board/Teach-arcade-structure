Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What does the community give up in exchange for apparent security?

  • Explain how memory shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce dystopia, euphemism, memory, and choice. Preview the infant-release sequence. Supply a novel passage and note that the film ages up the characters and changes its visual treatment.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • An infant is killed by injection in a disturbing sequence.
  • Memories of war, injury, death, and intense emotional distress.
  • Threats, pursuit, and a young child in danger.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the assignment ceremony, after a significant memory, after Jonas learns about release, and before the boundary crossing.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The setting is invented, not an account of a real society. Distinguish a promise of safety from its costs. The film’s color transition offers a way to examine how a medium makes abstract ideas visible.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

AFI film credits · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What does the community give up in exchange for apparent security?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What can a euphemism conceal?
    A mild term can hide a harmful or difficult reality from scrutiny.
  2. How does the opening portray sameness?
    Controlled behavior, routines, and limited visible color suggest a regulated life.
  3. What happens at the assignment ceremony?
    Adults determine the young people’s roles; Jonas is selected as Receiver of Memory.
  4. Why does his assignment isolate him?
    He gains access to experiences and rules that others do not share.
  5. How does color change as Jonas learns?
    Expanding color makes his changing perception visible to viewers.
  6. Why are pleasant memories not the whole story?
    Understanding human experience also exposes suffering and conflict.
  7. How does knowledge change Jonas’s conversations with others?
    He recognizes meanings and losses that their regulated experience does not allow them to understand.
  8. How does the revelation about release change Jonas’s judgment?
    He discovers that reassuring language conceals deliberate killing.
  9. What does the Chief Elder fear about restoring memories?
    She sees emotion and choice as risks to the order she protects.
  10. What does the ending ask us to consider about memory?
    Returning experience makes genuine feeling and choice possible while reintroducing pain.
  11. Which lost freedom matters most to the film’s argument?
    Choose a specific freedom and support its importance with scenes.
  12. How does a selected Lowry passage differ from its adaptation?
    Compare character age, narration, ambiguity, or visual emphasis using evidence.
  13. What should remain uncertain when interpreting the ending?
    Separate what is shown from assumptions about how the community’s future will develop.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What can a euphemism conceal?
    A mild term can hide a harmful or difficult reality from scrutiny.
  2. What makes a society dystopian despite an orderly appearance?
    Its stability may depend on control, exclusion, or the loss of important freedoms.
  3. How does the opening portray sameness?
    Controlled behavior, routines, and limited visible color suggest a regulated life.
  4. What happens at the assignment ceremony?
    Adults determine the young people’s roles; Jonas is selected as Receiver of Memory.
  5. Why does his assignment isolate him?
    He gains access to experiences and rules that others do not share.
  6. What does the Giver transfer through memories?
    Jonas experiences a wider human past, including beauty, emotion, and pain.
  7. How does color change as Jonas learns?
    Expanding color makes his changing perception visible to viewers.
  8. Why are pleasant memories not the whole story?
    Understanding human experience also exposes suffering and conflict.
  9. How does knowledge change Jonas’s conversations with others?
    He recognizes meanings and losses that their regulated experience does not allow them to understand.
  10. What does his relationship with Gabriel reveal?
    Care for an individual challenges the community’s impersonal rules.
  11. How does the revelation about release change Jonas’s judgment?
    He discovers that reassuring language conceals deliberate killing.
  12. Why does his father’s role make the discovery more unsettling?
    A trusted caregiver performs an act Jonas now recognizes as harmful.
  13. What does the Chief Elder fear about restoring memories?
    She sees emotion and choice as risks to the order she protects.
  14. Why does Jonas leave with Gabriel?
    He seeks to protect the child and change what the community can know.
  15. What obstacles make the escape uncertain?
    Pursuit, exposure, and the difficulty of crossing the boundary create danger.
  16. What does the ending ask us to consider about memory?
    Returning experience makes genuine feeling and choice possible while reintroducing pain.
  17. Which lost freedom matters most to the film’s argument?
    Choose a specific freedom and support its importance with scenes.
  18. How does one visual transition communicate an idea?
    Explain the effect of color, lighting, or framing at a particular moment.
  19. How does a selected Lowry passage differ from its adaptation?
    Compare character age, narration, ambiguity, or visual emphasis using evidence.
  20. What should remain uncertain when interpreting the ending?
    Separate what is shown from assumptions about how the community’s future will develop.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
