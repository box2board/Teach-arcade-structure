Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How can competition and care coexist in the relationship the film presents?

  • Distinguish filmed observations and participant accounts from interpretation.
  • Support an analysis with two accurately described moments from this documentary.
  • Identify how selection, editing, or missing perspectives limit a conclusion.

Introduce practice, feedback, rivalry, documentary selection, and accessibility. Use respectful language about autism and avoid asking students to disclose diagnoses. Cubing is not portrayed as a universal treatment or measure of worth.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Discussion of developmental challenges and family worries.
  • Competition disappointment and emotionally intense interviews.
  • Accounts of autism and family concerns; discuss the individuals respectfully without requiring personal disclosure.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on participants; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the introductions, after the family account of learning, before the championship final, and after the competitors’ responses.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The film includes family accounts of Max’s experiences and selected competition moments. Distinguish those accounts from conclusions about all autistic people. Give weight to the participants’ own actions and stated preferences; the film is not a diagnostic resource.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How can competition and care coexist in the relationship the film presents?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What can deliberate practice add beyond repeating an activity?
    It uses goals and feedback to improve a specific skill.
  2. How is Feliks introduced within speedcubing?
    The film establishes his achievements and role in the community.
  3. How is Max’s developing skill presented?
    Practice and competition footage show rapid growth alongside personal and family accounts.
  4. What do the family interviews contribute?
    They provide context and interpretation that timed results alone do not offer.
  5. How does meeting other cubers affect the story?
    The community offers relationships, models, and opportunities beyond solitary practice.
  6. What makes Feliks important to Max beyond his record?
    The film presents him as an admired person and supportive competitor.
  7. How does their relationship challenge a simple rivalry story?
    Friendly actions and encouragement coexist with serious competition.
  8. How do records shape the documentary’s suspense?
    They provide measurable stakes while leaving future outcomes uncertain.
  9. How does the championship bring different expectations together?
    Personal hopes, competitive pressure, and relationships converge in one event.
  10. What does the conclusion emphasize beyond speed?
    The film gives relationships and participation meaning alongside achievement.
  11. Which scene best shows respectful competition?
    Describe the action and explain how it treats another person.
  12. What claim would require another source or participant perspective?
    Choose a question about training, access, or experience that this edited film cannot settle.
  13. Why should students avoid treating cubing as a cure for autism?
    The film presents individual experiences, not a universal medical intervention.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What can deliberate practice add beyond repeating an activity?
    It uses goals and feedback to improve a specific skill.
  2. Why can competitors also care about each other?
    A shared interest and respect can support relationships despite conflicting results.
  3. How is Feliks introduced within speedcubing?
    The film establishes his achievements and role in the community.
  4. How is Max’s developing skill presented?
    Practice and competition footage show rapid growth alongside personal and family accounts.
  5. What do the family interviews contribute?
    They provide context and interpretation that timed results alone do not offer.
  6. Why should Max’s experiences not stand for every autistic person?
    People differ, and this documentary follows one selected individual and family.
  7. How does meeting other cubers affect the story?
    The community offers relationships, models, and opportunities beyond solitary practice.
  8. What makes Feliks important to Max beyond his record?
    The film presents him as an admired person and supportive competitor.
  9. How does their relationship challenge a simple rivalry story?
    Friendly actions and encouragement coexist with serious competition.
  10. What does a preparation scene reveal about practice?
    Identify a specific routine, concentration, or adjustment rather than assuming success is effortless.
  11. How do records shape the documentary’s suspense?
    They provide measurable stakes while leaving future outcomes uncertain.
  12. What does reaction footage show that a stopwatch cannot?
    It records participants’ responses and social interaction around results.
  13. How does the championship bring different expectations together?
    Personal hopes, competitive pressure, and relationships converge in one event.
  14. What response to disappointment does the film show?
    Describe a participant’s observed action or statement without inventing a diagnosis.
  15. How does support after a result complicate winning and losing?
    Care continues even when the outcome does not favor both competitors.
  16. What does the conclusion emphasize beyond speed?
    The film gives relationships and participation meaning alongside achievement.
  17. Which scene best shows respectful competition?
    Describe the action and explain how it treats another person.
  18. How does music or editing guide our response to a timed solve?
    Identify a specific technique and distinguish its emotional effect from the measured result.
  19. What claim would require another source or participant perspective?
    Choose a question about training, access, or experience that this edited film cannot settle.
  20. Why should students avoid treating cubing as a cure for autism?
    The film presents individual experiences, not a universal medical intervention.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
