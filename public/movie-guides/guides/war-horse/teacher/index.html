Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does War Horse reveal the costs of WWI, and what can fiction tell us about a real conflict?

  • Explain how modern weapons change the experiences of soldiers and horses.
  • Use scenes to explain how war affects soldiers, civilians, and animals.
  • Distinguish a film’s portrayal from a claim supported by historical evidence.

Introduce the alliances, machine guns, trenches, cavalry, artillery, and no man’s land. The paper worksheet is a shorter assignment; the online version offers more analysis. You can assign selected questions.

Content considerations & viewing plan

Preview the film’s battles, injuries, deaths, separation, and trauma. Provide context and time for discussion.

Questions follow the story: the farm; Joey’s military service; German soldiers and civilians; artillery and no man’s land; and reunion. Choose stopping points after previewing your edition. These sections are not equal-length class periods.

Use brief answers during viewing. Save extended writing for afterward so students can focus on the film.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does Joey’s movement between opposing sides change our perspective?
  • What does the rescue in no man’s land reveal about compassion? What does it leave unresolved?
  • Which detail should we investigate before treating the film as representative of WWI?

Exit ticket: One scene, one insight about war, and one historical question.

Optional source comparison: Supply a verified wartime photograph, letter, or record about military horses. Compare what it establishes with a scene. Source selection is required before assigning this extension.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for the 13-question PDF. Accept supported alternatives for open-ended questions.

  1. War can disrupt homes, food supplies, families, work, or safety.
  2. Joey helps plow the farm and supports the family’s livelihood.
  3. Albert is separated from his companion as war redirects Joey into military service.
  4. Machine-gun fire devastates the exposed cavalry charge.
  5. The older brother leaves with his younger brother to protect him; they are caught and executed.
  6. Military activity disrupts their home and their relationship with the horses.
  7. They pull artillery; exhaustion and Topthorn’s death show its physical cost.
  8. Joey runs into barbed wire in no man’s land.
  9. Soldiers from opposing sides work together to cut Joey free.
  10. Albert uses his familiar call and recognizes Joey’s markings.
  11. The grandfather recognizes Albert’s bond with Joey, who also connects him to Emilie.
  12. Accept a specific scene and a supported explanation of personal loss, civilian disruption, or destructive warfare.
  13. Accept a relevant claim and a suitable source, such as military records, letters, photographs, or a scholarly history.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for the 20-question online worksheet. Accept supported interpretations backed by scenes.

  1. Connect prior knowledge to a plausible effect on people.
  2. Joey crosses national and social boundaries, revealing shared experiences.
  3. Joey helps work the farm and supports the family’s livelihood.
  4. Albert’s patient training, familiar call, or plowing with Joey shows their bond.
  5. Joey’s sale separates Albert from him before Albert enters combat.
  6. Nicholls takes Albert seriously and promises to care for Joey.
  7. Machine guns devastate the exposed charge; avoid generalizing to every cavalry role.
  8. Viewers encounter German soldiers and their relationships with the horses.
  9. The older brother leaves with the younger brother; they are caught and executed.
  10. Soldiers’ presence and demands disrupt civilian life.
  11. Military authorities take resources civilians value or depend on.
  12. They haul artillery; exhaustion, strain, and Topthorn’s death show the cost.
  13. Joey runs into barbed wire in the exposed battlefield.
  14. Opposing soldiers cut Joey free, sharing concern despite being enemies.
  15. Albert uses his call and recognizes Joey’s markings.
  16. Joey connects the grandfather to Emilie; recognizing Albert’s bond leads him to relinquish Joey.
  17. Explain the mismatch between exposed mounted tactics and machine-gun defenses.
  18. Identify two people and support a shared experience or value with scenes.
  19. Cooperation demonstrates compassion without ending the wider conflict or obligations.
  20. Identify a historical claim and an appropriate source to investigate it.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, accurate evidence from two scenes, reasoning, and one limitation of film as historical evidence. Interpretations may vary when supported.
