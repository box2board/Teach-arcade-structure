War Horse Movie Guide

A classroom-ready guide to War Horse that blends World War I history with themes of courage, compassion, and human–animal relationships. Use it to anchor discussion, writing, and primary source work.

Subject Area: World History / WWI / Literature Adaptation Grade Levels: Middle School, High School Focus Topics: Courage, Survival, Human–Animal Bonds

Print-friendly: All sections are designed for easy handouts or slides.

Teacher Snapshot / Movie Details

Movie Details

  • Release Date: 2011
  • Cast: Jeremy Irvine, Emily Watson, Peter Mullan, Benedict Cumberbatch, Tom Hiddleston
  • Director: Steven Spielberg
  • Genre: War Drama, Historical Fiction
  • Run Time: 146 minutes
  • Rating: PG-13

Teacher Review

War Horse tells the emotional story of Joey and the many people he touches during World War I. Spielberg portrays the brutality of war alongside the hope found in loyalty and compassion. The film is an excellent resource for teaching history through personal stories.

Summary

Young Albert Narracott bonds deeply with a horse named Joey. When Joey is sold to the army during World War I, the film follows the horse’s journey across battlefields and through the hands of soldiers, farmers, and families until an emotional reunion brings hope amid devastation.

Use in the Classroom

Ideal for World History, English Literature, and Ethics discussions. The film provides historical insight into WWI, highlights compassion amid conflict, and supports adaptation discussions (novel to film).

Content Considerations

  • Battle scenes with realistic war violence.
  • Emotional themes involving injury, death, and separation.

Guiding Questions

  1. Who is Albert Narracott, and what is his relationship with Joey?
  2. Why does Albert’s father buy Joey despite their financial troubles?
  3. How does Joey’s training reflect the bond between human and animal?
  4. What events lead to Joey being sold to the army?
  5. How does the film depict trench warfare and the brutality of WWI?
  6. Who are some of the people Joey encounters during the war?
  7. How is the Red Cross portrayed in the film?
  8. What role do horses play in WWI as shown in the movie?
  9. How do themes of loyalty and survival appear throughout the story?
  10. How does the cinematography convey the emotions of the story?
  11. What sacrifices do characters make in the film?
  12. How does Joey’s journey symbolize resilience?
  13. What does the film suggest about hope and compassion during wartime?
  14. How are class divisions depicted in the relationships among characters?
  15. What lessons can modern audiences learn from War Horse?

Primary Source Connection

Document: Excerpts from WWI soldier diaries discussing the use of horses in battle.

Activity: Compare soldiers’ descriptions of war horses to how Joey’s story is portrayed.

  • What details about horses’ roles are similar in the diaries and the film?
  • What emotions do the soldiers express that echo Joey’s journey?
  • What differences do you notice between historical accounts and the film’s storytelling?

Post-Viewing Reflection

Prompt: In what ways do compassion and loyalty survive even during times of terrible conflict?

Socratic / Critical Thinking Questions

  • What is the emotional cost of war for both people and animals?
  • How does loyalty motivate characters to act courageously?
  • How do small acts of kindness have large impacts during wartime?
  • Can animals teach humans important lessons about empathy?

Teacher Answer Key

Guiding Questions Sample Answers

  1. Albert Narracott is a farmer’s son who raises Joey; their relationship is built on trust and devotion.
  2. He buys Joey hoping the horse can help work the farm and prove his determination despite hardship.
  3. Joey learns commands through patience and care, showing mutual respect between animal and handler.
  4. Financial pressure and the war effort lead the family to sell Joey to the army.
  5. The film shows muddy trenches, artillery fire, and chaos to emphasize the brutality of WWI.
  6. Joey meets British soldiers, French farmers, German troops, and Red Cross staff.
  7. The Red Cross appears as a humanitarian force offering care and neutrality amid conflict.
  8. Horses serve in cavalry units, haul supplies, and represent the cost of war on animals.
  9. Loyalty appears in characters risking safety for Joey; survival is shown in constant struggle to endure.
  10. Cinematography uses sweeping landscapes and close-ups to heighten emotion and empathy.
  11. Characters sacrifice safety, comfort, and even life to protect others or follow duty.
  12. Joey survives multiple environments and owners, symbolizing perseverance through hardship.
  13. The film suggests hope is sustained through small acts of compassion.
  14. Class differences appear in tensions between landowners, farmers, and soldiers of different ranks.
  15. Modern audiences can learn about empathy, the cost of war, and the importance of compassion.
