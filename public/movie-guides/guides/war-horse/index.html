War Horse Movie Guide (Teacher-Friendly)

A classroom-ready viewing guide for War Horse that emphasizes empathy, historical context, and the human cost of World War I. This guide is designed for students and keeps the tone teacher-safe while still highlighting key themes.

Best fit: Grades 7–12 Themes: empathy, war, resilience Subject: Social Studies

Download / Print: This guide is printer-friendly — copy and paste into your lesson plan or slides.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how World War I impacted civilians and soldiers across different nations.
  • Identify examples of resilience, loyalty, and moral courage in the film.
  • Analyze how perspective and point of view influence historical narratives.
  • Connect film scenes to real-world WWI themes and historical context.

Before You Watch (5 Questions)

  1. What do you already know about World War I and the Western Front?
  2. How can animals become part of human conflicts, even when they have no choice?
  3. What does loyalty look like in a time of crisis?
  4. What makes a story feel “historical” rather than just fictional?
  5. Which perspectives might be missing from a typical war story?

While You Watch (Guiding Prompts)

  1. Notice how Albert and Joey’s bond is shown without many words. What details communicate their relationship?
  2. Track how the war affects people from different countries. What stays the same across their experiences?
  3. What choices do characters make that show courage or compassion?
  4. How does the film show the impact of war on rural families and farms?
  5. Look for scenes that show hope even in difficult situations.
  6. When do characters show empathy toward someone on the “other side”?
  7. How does the setting (trenches, fields, towns) shape the mood of each scene?
  8. What sacrifices do characters make, and why?
  9. Pay attention to how time passes in the film. What clues show the length of the war?
  10. Which scenes feel most historically grounded? Which feel more symbolic or emotional?
  11. How does Joey’s journey help viewers understand the scale of the war?
  12. What symbols or repeated images stand out (fields, fences, uniforms, animals)?

After You Watch (Discussion Questions)

  1. How does the film portray the costs of war for families, not just soldiers?
  2. What moments in the film felt historically accurate, and which felt more dramatic or symbolic?
  3. How did the relationship between Albert and Joey drive the story?
  4. Which character changed the most, and why?
  5. How does the film help viewers understand the scale of World War I?
  6. What does the movie suggest about empathy across national lines?
  7. How would the story change if told from a different character’s viewpoint?
  8. What responsibilities do people have to animals during wartime?
  9. Which scene stayed with you the most? Why?
  10. How does the ending connect to the film’s overall message?

Extension Activities

  • Writing Prompt: Write a journal entry from the perspective of a WWI civilian who hears news from the front.
  • Primary Source Tie-In: Compare a wartime letter or photo with a similar scene in the film. What matches? What differs?
  • SEL Reflection: Identify a moment of empathy in the film and connect it to a time you saw kindness in real life.

Quick Exit Ticket (3 Prompts)

  • One new thing I learned about World War I is…
  • A moment that showed resilience was…
  • A question I still have is…

Answer Key / Teacher Notes

Suggested responses:

  • Students should recognize the broad impact of war on families, farms, and civilians. Answers will vary.
  • Examples of empathy include moments of care between soldiers, farmers, and animals. Answers will vary.
  • Historical context includes trenches, uniforms, and wartime displacement; some scenes are dramatized for storytelling.
  • Point of view matters: the film leans on Joey’s journey, but other perspectives can shift interpretations.
