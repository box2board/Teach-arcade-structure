Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How do relationships help Vada understand change and loss?

  • Explain how friendship shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Preview the friend’s death and funeral scenes. Offer an alternative and allow students to discuss fictional characters without sharing personal losses. Introduce perspective, inference, and supportive communication.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • A child dies following bee stings; the funeral includes a body-viewing scene and intense distress.
  • A funeral-home setting, parental death, and repeated discussion of illness and dying.
  • Puberty, mild sexual references, some language, and family conflict.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after Shelly joins the household, after the poetry-class sequence, before the death is discussed, and after the final reflection.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The story portrays one child’s experience, not a fixed sequence of grief stages. Thomas J.’s death involves a severe allergic reaction; keep the discussion on narrative and support rather than using the film as health instruction.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How do relationships help Vada understand change and loss?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a story reveal a feeling without naming it directly?
    Behavior, dialogue, silence, and reactions can provide evidence for an interpretation.
  2. How does living in a funeral home shape Vada’s world?
    Death and adult work are part of her daily surroundings, affecting her questions and worries.
  3. What does Vada’s attention to illness suggest?
    It shows worry and an attempt to understand her experience; the film does not establish a diagnosis.
  4. How does Thomas J. show that he values their friendship?
    He spends time with Vada and responds to her interests and concerns.
  5. How does Shelly gradually build trust?
    She offers attention and practical support rather than demanding immediate acceptance.
  6. Why does Vada join the poetry class?
    Her interest is connected to admiration for the teacher and a desire to be noticed.
  7. What does her response to the teacher’s relationship reveal?
    She has interpreted the situation through her own hopes and must revise that understanding.
  8. How does Vada react to her father’s developing relationship?
    She resists a change that seems to threaten her existing family connection.
  9. How does Vada initially respond to the news of his death?
    Her reactions show shock and difficulty comprehending the loss rather than a single orderly response.
  10. What changes in Vada’s final reflection?
    She is better able to acknowledge feeling and connection while the loss remains significant.
  11. Which scene shows growth in a relationship?
    Describe a specific change in communication or trust and support it with evidence.
  12. How does a film technique shape our response to one moment?
    Explain the role of music, framing, performance, or silence in a specific scene.
  13. What would a caring response to a fictional grieving classmate include?
    Offer listening, choices, and access to trusted support without demanding disclosure or promising to fix the loss.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a story reveal a feeling without naming it directly?
    Behavior, dialogue, silence, and reactions can provide evidence for an interpretation.
  2. What distinguishes a supportive response from telling someone how to feel?
    Support listens and offers care without demanding a particular emotion or timetable.
  3. How does living in a funeral home shape Vada’s world?
    Death and adult work are part of her daily surroundings, affecting her questions and worries.
  4. What does Vada’s attention to illness suggest?
    It shows worry and an attempt to understand her experience; the film does not establish a diagnosis.
  5. How does Thomas J. show that he values their friendship?
    He spends time with Vada and responds to her interests and concerns.
  6. Why does Shelly’s arrival unsettle Vada?
    A new adult relationship changes routines and Vada’s sense of her place in the family.
  7. How does Shelly gradually build trust?
    She offers attention and practical support rather than demanding immediate acceptance.
  8. Why does Vada join the poetry class?
    Her interest is connected to admiration for the teacher and a desire to be noticed.
  9. What does her response to the teacher’s relationship reveal?
    She has interpreted the situation through her own hopes and must revise that understanding.
  10. How do Vada and Thomas J.’s conversations show both closeness and uncertainty?
    They share experiences while still trying to understand growing up and changing feelings.
  11. How does Vada react to her father’s developing relationship?
    She resists a change that seems to threaten her existing family connection.
  12. What does Thomas J.’s search for the lost ring reveal about his intentions?
    It connects his effort to care for Vada with the tragic event that follows.
  13. How does Vada initially respond to the news of his death?
    Her reactions show shock and difficulty comprehending the loss rather than a single orderly response.
  14. Why is her response at the funeral especially painful?
    She confronts the difference between the friend she knew and the reality of his death.
  15. How can adults support Vada without erasing her loss?
    They can listen, remain available, and give clear, gentle explanations without rushing her.
  16. What changes in Vada’s final reflection?
    She is better able to acknowledge feeling and connection while the loss remains significant.
  17. Which scene shows growth in a relationship?
    Describe a specific change in communication or trust and support it with evidence.
  18. Why should viewers avoid treating Vada’s grief as a universal pattern?
    People’s responses and circumstances differ; one fictional portrayal cannot prescribe everyone’s experience.
  19. How does a film technique shape our response to one moment?
    Explain the role of music, framing, performance, or silence in a specific scene.
  20. What would a caring response to a fictional grieving classmate include?
    Offer listening, choices, and access to trusted support without demanding disclosure or promising to fix the loss.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
