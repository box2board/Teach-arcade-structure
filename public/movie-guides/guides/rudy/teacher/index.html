Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How do persistence and support interact in Rudy’s pursuit of his goal?

  • Explain how persistence shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce goal, feedback, support, and dramatization. Avoid presenting effort as a guarantee of admission or success. Use a teacher-selected independent biographical source when evaluating historical claims.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • A workplace accident kills a friend; grief and dangerous physical activity.
  • Football impacts, injury risk, some language, drinking, and smoking.
  • Academic frustration, rejection, and family conflict; avoid ranking students’ worth by achievement.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after Rudy leaves home, after the study-support sequence, after the admission decision, and before the final game.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The film condenses a real life and uses dramatic scenes to organize its message. Distinguish what the film depicts from what an external source verifies; do not assume that dialogue or team gestures are documented exactly.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How do persistence and support interact in Rudy’s pursuit of his goal?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes a goal specific enough to evaluate?
    It states a concrete outcome and allows progress or obstacles to be identified.
  2. How does Rudy’s family respond to his ambition?
    Some doubt its practicality, establishing tension between expectation and his chosen goal.
  3. What does his factory work reveal about his starting circumstances?
    He is expected to follow a familiar working life rather than an easy path into college athletics.
  4. How does his friend’s death influence his decision?
    The loss contributes to urgency about pursuing the future he has delayed.
  5. What role does Holy Cross play in his plan?
    It gives him a route to improve academic standing and pursue later admission to Notre Dame.
  6. How does D-Bob help Rudy?
    Study support and recognition of learning difficulties help him approach school differently.
  7. Why is asking for help compatible with persistence?
    It can change an ineffective approach instead of repeating the same obstacle alone.
  8. What does admission change, and what does it leave unresolved?
    He reaches the academic institution but still must seek a place and opportunity on the team.
  9. How does Fortune challenge Rudy’s interpretation of success?
    He encourages Rudy to recognize what he has accomplished rather than see only an unmet game goal.
  10. Why does the final game matter within the film’s structure?
    It provides a visible culmination of the long pursuit and a public moment of recognition.
  11. Which scene shows a useful change in strategy rather than effort alone?
    Explain a supported example such as tutoring, academic preparation, or accepting feedback.
  12. How does music or editing shape the final game’s impact?
    Connect a specific film choice to anticipation, emphasis, or the viewer’s response.
  13. How would you test one historical claim made by the dramatization?
    Name a depicted event and identify an independent source needed to check it; distinguish uncertainty from proof.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes a goal specific enough to evaluate?
    It states a concrete outcome and allows progress or obstacles to be identified.
  2. How can support change what persistence makes possible?
    Resources, instruction, relationships, and access can help effort produce useful progress.
  3. How does Rudy’s family respond to his ambition?
    Some doubt its practicality, establishing tension between expectation and his chosen goal.
  4. What does his factory work reveal about his starting circumstances?
    He is expected to follow a familiar working life rather than an easy path into college athletics.
  5. How does his friend’s death influence his decision?
    The loss contributes to urgency about pursuing the future he has delayed.
  6. Why does reaching Notre Dame require more than enthusiasm?
    Admission, academic preparation, work, and athletic requirements create distinct barriers.
  7. What role does Holy Cross play in his plan?
    It gives him a route to improve academic standing and pursue later admission to Notre Dame.
  8. How does D-Bob help Rudy?
    Study support and recognition of learning difficulties help him approach school differently.
  9. Why is asking for help compatible with persistence?
    It can change an ineffective approach instead of repeating the same obstacle alone.
  10. How do rejection letters affect the story’s pacing?
    Repeated setbacks delay the goal and make progress uncertain rather than immediate.
  11. What does admission change, and what does it leave unresolved?
    He reaches the academic institution but still must seek a place and opportunity on the team.
  12. How does practice show a different kind of contribution?
    Preparation and effort can support a team even without public game recognition.
  13. How does Fortune challenge Rudy’s interpretation of success?
    He encourages Rudy to recognize what he has accomplished rather than see only an unmet game goal.
  14. Why does Rudy consider leaving the team?
    Frustration grows when effort does not appear to lead to the opportunity he wants.
  15. What does teammates’ support contribute in the film?
    It makes recognition collective, though the scene’s historical details require external verification.
  16. Why does the final game matter within the film’s structure?
    It provides a visible culmination of the long pursuit and a public moment of recognition.
  17. Which scene shows a useful change in strategy rather than effort alone?
    Explain a supported example such as tutoring, academic preparation, or accepting feedback.
  18. What opportunity or resource could the film’s message understate?
    Identify access, money, instruction, health, or social support and explain its importance.
  19. How does music or editing shape the final game’s impact?
    Connect a specific film choice to anticipation, emphasis, or the viewer’s response.
  20. How would you test one historical claim made by the dramatization?
    Name a depicted event and identify an independent source needed to check it; distinguish uncertainty from proof.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
