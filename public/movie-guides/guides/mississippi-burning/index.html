Teacher Guide – Mississippi Burning

A classroom-ready guide to Mississippi Burning that supports Civil Rights instruction while examining federalism, law enforcement, and the moral complexity of justice in the Jim Crow South.

Subject Area: U.S. History / Civil Rights / Government / Social Justice Grade Levels: High School (Mature Themes) Focus Topics: Civil Rights Movement, Racism, Justice, Federalism, Law Enforcement

Print-friendly: Student sections are styled for easy handouts or slides.

Movie Details

  • Release Date: 1988
  • Director: Alan Parker
  • Starring: Gene Hackman, Willem Dafoe, Frances McDormand
  • Genre: Historical Crime Drama
  • Runtime: 128 minutes
  • Rating: R

Summary

Set in 1964 Mississippi, the film follows two FBI agents investigating the disappearance of three civil rights workers. As they face hostility from the local community and officials, they uncover systemic racism and violent resistance to integration. The film portrays the brutal reality of the Jim Crow South and the challenges of enforcing civil rights laws.

Use in the Classroom

  • U.S. History: Civil Rights Movement
  • Government: Federal vs. State authority
  • Civics: Legal systems, justice, and law enforcement
  • Social Studies: Race, ethics, and institutional discrimination

Content Considerations

  • Racial slurs and violence.
  • Depictions of lynching, burning crosses, and physical abuse.
  • Intense emotional content — recommended for mature students with proper context and support.

Pre-Viewing Prompt

Prompt: What is the role of the federal government in protecting individual rights when local institutions are complicit in injustice?

Guiding Questions

  1. What event sparks the FBI investigation in the film?
  2. How are the two FBI agents portrayed differently?
  3. Why does the local community resist cooperation?
  4. What does the burning of churches symbolize?
  5. How is Sheriff Stuckey’s role in the system shown?
  6. What risks do local Black citizens face in speaking out?
  7. How do the agents’ methods differ in seeking justice?
  8. What motivates the informant to help the investigation?
  9. How does the film depict the complicity of law enforcement?
  10. What role does fear play in the town’s behavior?
  11. How is Mrs. Pell’s character significant?
  12. What message is sent when the FBI uses aggressive tactics?
  13. How does the community react to outside intervention?
  14. What broader message does the film deliver about justice?
  15. How does this story connect to ongoing civil rights challenges today?

Primary Source Connection

Document: FBI files and congressional records from the real 1964 Freedom Summer murders.

Activity: Compare film portrayals with real documents; examine bias, accuracy, and dramatic license.

  • Highlight details that are supported by primary sources.
  • Identify scenes that appear dramatized or simplified.
  • Discuss how filmmakers balance truth-telling and storytelling.

Visual Project Options

  • Create a timeline of Freedom Summer and the FBI investigation.
  • Design a civil rights newspaper front page.
  • Illustrate a symbolic scene from the movie showing fear, hope, or justice.

Post-Viewing Reflection

Prompt: How should institutions respond when the law protects injustice rather than people?

Critical Thinking Questions

  • When does silence become complicity?
  • Is justice ever served through unlawful means?
  • How should federal agents act when local systems are corrupt?
