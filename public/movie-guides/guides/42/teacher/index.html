Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What does the film reveal about challenging discrimination within an institution?

  • Explain how institutional discrimination shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce segregation, discrimination, institution, and allyship. Explain that Robinson broke the modern major leagues’ color barrier in 1947; Black players had participated in earlier professional baseball. Do not repeat racial slurs or stage abuse simulations.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Frequent racial slurs, harassment, threats, and discriminatory treatment.
  • Baseball-related injury and intense emotional distress.
  • Language, drinking, and some sexual references.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the contract discussion, after the team petition, after the Philadelphia abuse, and after a public act of support.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

Verify specific events through a teacher-selected baseball archive or Robinson source. The film concentrates a broader struggle into selected scenes and characters; Branch Rickey’s support does not replace Robinson’s agency or the work of others.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What does the film reveal about challenging discrimination within an institution?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can discrimination operate through rules as well as insults?
    Policies and unequal access can exclude people even without an individual insult.
  2. Why does Rickey seek to sign Robinson?
    He challenges the color barrier while pursuing a player who can succeed under intense pressure.
  3. What difficult expectation does Rickey place on him?
    Robinson is asked not to retaliate against provocation while facing discriminatory treatment.
  4. How does Rachel’s presence matter to the story?
    She offers support and shares the consequences of hostile treatment and public attention.
  5. How does the team petition expose resistance within baseball?
    Some players organize to reject a teammate on racial grounds.
  6. Why is leadership’s response to the petition important?
    An institution can enable exclusion or refuse to give it official legitimacy.
  7. How does the Philadelphia manager use abuse?
    He attempts to provoke and undermine Robinson through racist harassment.
  8. How does Rickey respond to Robinson’s frustration?
    He reaffirms the stakes while helping him return to the field.
  9. What do hostile injuries and threats reveal about the barrier?
    Resistance involves physical danger as well as words or formal exclusion.
  10. How does the ending frame his significance?
    It connects his season with a broader legacy, though the film cannot cover the entire struggle.
  11. Which scene most clearly shows institutional discrimination?
    Identify a rule or practice and explain its effect on access or treatment.
  12. What depicted event would you verify against an archival source?
    Select a specific scene, name a suitable source, and separate depiction from confirmation.
  13. What does the film leave out of baseball’s racial history?
    Earlier Black professional players, the Negro Leagues, and continuing inequities require additional study.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can discrimination operate through rules as well as insults?
    Policies and unequal access can exclude people even without an individual insult.
  2. What makes a public act of support meaningful?
    It can challenge accepted conduct and carry consequences for the person offering it.
  3. Why does Rickey seek to sign Robinson?
    He challenges the color barrier while pursuing a player who can succeed under intense pressure.
  4. What difficult expectation does Rickey place on him?
    Robinson is asked not to retaliate against provocation while facing discriminatory treatment.
  5. How does Rachel’s presence matter to the story?
    She offers support and shares the consequences of hostile treatment and public attention.
  6. What does the travel experience reveal about segregation?
    Ordinary services and accommodation become barriers because access is unequal.
  7. How does the team petition expose resistance within baseball?
    Some players organize to reject a teammate on racial grounds.
  8. Why is leadership’s response to the petition important?
    An institution can enable exclusion or refuse to give it official legitimacy.
  9. How does the Philadelphia manager use abuse?
    He attempts to provoke and undermine Robinson through racist harassment.
  10. What does the private distress scene add to the public image?
    It shows the emotional cost concealed by expectations of composure.
  11. How does Rickey respond to Robinson’s frustration?
    He reaffirms the stakes while helping him return to the field.
  12. How does a teammate’s visible support affect the moment?
    It rejects isolation and makes solidarity observable to spectators.
  13. What do hostile injuries and threats reveal about the barrier?
    Resistance involves physical danger as well as words or formal exclusion.
  14. How does Robinson’s play challenge assumptions?
    His performance contradicts racist claims without making talent a condition for equal dignity.
  15. Why is change not only an individual achievement?
    Decisions by teammates, managers, supporters, and institutions affect the opportunities available.
  16. How does the ending frame his significance?
    It connects his season with a broader legacy, though the film cannot cover the entire struggle.
  17. Which scene most clearly shows institutional discrimination?
    Identify a rule or practice and explain its effect on access or treatment.
  18. How can support avoid overshadowing the person facing discrimination?
    Recognize the person’s agency and describe supporters’ contributions accurately.
  19. What depicted event would you verify against an archival source?
    Select a specific scene, name a suitable source, and separate depiction from confirmation.
  20. What does the film leave out of baseball’s racial history?
    Earlier Black professional players, the Negro Leagues, and continuing inequities require additional study.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
