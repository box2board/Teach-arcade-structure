Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How do high expectations become meaningful when students also receive access and support?

  • Identify concrete practices and supports behind progress.
  • Analyze the difference between fair scrutiny and prejudiced assumptions.
  • Evaluate the limits of a film centered on one teacher.

Introduce AP coursework, calculus, prerequisite knowledge, evidence, and educational opportunity. No calculus background is needed to analyze the learning process. Students should study the portrayed class rather than compare their grades or family circumstances with it. Discuss humiliating or suggestive teaching remarks critically rather than endorsing them.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • English and Spanish profanity, sexual jokes, and inappropriate comments about female students.
  • Threats, school conflict, gang-related pressure, and a teacher’s health crisis.
  • Smoking, drinking, financial pressures, and stressful accusations of cheating.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after Escalante’s arrival, after the commitment to advanced preparation, after the disputed results, and after the retest.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The drama compresses a longer process of preparation and support. Consult the AFI history and a teacher-selected account of the real program rather than assuming one year or one charismatic teacher explains every result. A retest is not a general measure of students’ worth. Do not infer that struggling students lack desire or that structural barriers disappear with persistence.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How do high expectations become meaningful when students also receive access and support?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes a high expectation different from demanding success without support?
    A meaningful expectation includes instruction, time, resources, feedback, and access to the necessary preparation.
  2. What assignment does Escalante encounter when he arrives at Garfield?
    He expects computing work but teaches mathematics because the school lacks the anticipated equipment.
  3. How does he initially try to engage the class?
    He uses demonstrations, humor, direct challenges, and practical examples, though not every remark is appropriate.
  4. What low expectations does he confront about the students?
    Others doubt their readiness or ability to succeed in advanced mathematics.
  5. How do additional study sessions change the students’ routine?
    They require time outside normal class and a serious commitment that competes with other responsibilities.
  6. What pressures make participation difficult for some students?
    Family work, finances, peer expectations, and other responsibilities affect available time and support.
  7. Why does Ana’s family situation matter to her academic opportunity?
    Restaurant responsibilities and family expectations threaten her ability to continue; support and negotiation affect access.
  8. What does Escalante’s health crisis reveal about the workload?
    The demands affect him physically, showing that intensive commitment has costs and should not be idealized without limits.
  9. Why are the results challenged?
    Testing officials question similarities and suspect cheating; the class and teacher dispute the conclusion.
  10. What does the retest outcome establish within the story?
    Their successful performance supports recognition of their knowledge; it does not justify every accusation or tactic.
  11. Choose one concrete support that contributes to the students’ progress.
    Accept instruction, practice, feedback, extra time, family negotiation, or peer support, with a specific scene.
  12. Why is perseverance alone an incomplete explanation of achievement?
    Opportunity, preparation, resources, supportive relationships, and fair recognition also matter.
  13. What detail should you verify before using the film as program history?
    The length of preparation, actual roles, or exam sequence; consult AFI background and a reliable program account.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes a high expectation different from demanding success without support?
    A meaningful expectation includes instruction, time, resources, feedback, and access to the necessary preparation.
  2. How could an assumption about a school affect how achievement is interpreted?
    People may underestimate students or treat success as suspicious instead of evaluating evidence fairly.
  3. What assignment does Escalante encounter when he arrives at Garfield?
    He expects computing work but teaches mathematics because the school lacks the anticipated equipment.
  4. How does he initially try to engage the class?
    He uses demonstrations, humor, direct challenges, and practical examples, though not every remark is appropriate.
  5. What low expectations does he confront about the students?
    Others doubt their readiness or ability to succeed in advanced mathematics.
  6. What does his plan for calculus require beyond enthusiasm?
    Students need prerequisite skills and substantial sustained preparation before the exam.
  7. How do additional study sessions change the students’ routine?
    They require time outside normal class and a serious commitment that competes with other responsibilities.
  8. What pressures make participation difficult for some students?
    Family work, finances, peer expectations, and other responsibilities affect available time and support.
  9. Why does Ana’s family situation matter to her academic opportunity?
    Restaurant responsibilities and family expectations threaten her ability to continue; support and negotiation affect access.
  10. How does Angel’s behavior complicate others’ first impressions?
    He cares about learning while managing peer pressure, challenging a simple assumption that his appearance means indifference.
  11. What does Escalante’s health crisis reveal about the workload?
    The demands affect him physically, showing that intensive commitment has costs and should not be idealized without limits.
  12. What achievement follows the class’s first exam effort?
    The students receive passing AP calculus results in the film, marking the outcome of sustained preparation.
  13. Why are the results challenged?
    Testing officials question similarities and suspect cheating; the class and teacher dispute the conclusion.
  14. How do the accusations affect the students beyond a test score?
    They threaten recognition, confidence, and trust in whether their work will be judged fairly.
  15. Why do students agree to a retest?
    They want to demonstrate their knowledge and regain recognition despite the pressure and limited preparation time.
  16. What does the retest outcome establish within the story?
    Their successful performance supports recognition of their knowledge; it does not justify every accusation or tactic.
  17. Choose one concrete support that contributes to the students’ progress.
    Accept instruction, practice, feedback, extra time, family negotiation, or peer support, with a specific scene.
  18. How can the teacher-centered story hide other contributors?
    Students’ own work, families, school leadership, prior instruction, and program resources may receive less attention.
  19. Why is perseverance alone an incomplete explanation of achievement?
    Opportunity, preparation, resources, supportive relationships, and fair recognition also matter.
  20. What detail should you verify before using the film as program history?
    The length of preparation, actual roles, or exam sequence; consult AFI background and a reliable program account.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
