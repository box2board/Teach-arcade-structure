Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does Birdy seek agency within expectations she did not choose?

  • Explain how agency shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce arranged marriage, agency, historical fiction, and comic voice. Preview childbirth and sexual references. Provide a selected Cushman passage and a teacher-selected historical source.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Arranged marriage involving a young teenager; coercion and sexual references.
  • Menstruation, sexual innuendo, a stillbirth, distressing childbirth, and references to other deaths.
  • Caning, sword injury with blood, animal deaths, some language, crude humor, and drinking.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the household debts are established, after a rejected suitor, after a friendship changes, and during the final marriage conflict.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

Contemporary musical choices and humor make the past approachable but are artistic decisions. Avoid treating one fictional household as a complete account of medieval women’s lives. Discuss coercion without asking students about family experiences.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does Birdy seek agency within expectations she did not choose?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What does agency mean when choices are restricted?
    It is the ability to act and influence one’s life within or against constraints.
  2. How does Birdy’s voice introduce her view of the household?
    Her observations emphasize frustration, curiosity, and irreverence rather than obedience.
  3. Why does her father pursue a wealthy marriage for her?
    Household debts make her marriage seem like a route to financial rescue.
  4. How does Birdy’s position differ from that of boys around her?
    Gender and status limit the futures others consider appropriate for her.
  5. How does her mother’s experience complicate the family story?
    Pregnancy and household expectations reveal burdens that Birdy is still learning to understand.
  6. Why does Birdy’s admiration for George matter?
    It expresses her desire for an idealized alternative to the future being arranged.
  7. How do changing friendships challenge her perspective?
    Others’ relationships and decisions cannot always be organized around her wishes.
  8. How does money influence the treatment of marriage?
    Economic need can turn a personal relationship into a transaction.
  9. What makes the final proposed match particularly troubling?
    Birdy’s wishes and welfare are subordinated to the bargain adults have made.
  10. What does the ending suggest about family loyalty?
    Care can require adults to change plans and recognize the person affected by them.
  11. Which moment combines humor with a critique of power?
    Explain a specific scene and the expectation it exposes.
  12. How does a selected novel passage convey Birdy’s voice differently?
    Compare diary-style language with the film’s narration, performance, or imagery.
  13. What historical claim would need independent evidence?
    Select an issue involving marriage, class, or women’s opportunities rather than treating the plot as proof.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What does agency mean when choices are restricted?
    It is the ability to act and influence one’s life within or against constraints.
  2. How can humor expose a serious problem?
    Exaggeration or a surprising perspective can reveal the unfairness behind a familiar expectation.
  3. How does Birdy’s voice introduce her view of the household?
    Her observations emphasize frustration, curiosity, and irreverence rather than obedience.
  4. Why does her father pursue a wealthy marriage for her?
    Household debts make her marriage seem like a route to financial rescue.
  5. How does Birdy’s position differ from that of boys around her?
    Gender and status limit the futures others consider appropriate for her.
  6. What do her strategies against suitors reveal?
    She uses available wit and behavior to obstruct plans she cannot simply veto.
  7. How does her mother’s experience complicate the family story?
    Pregnancy and household expectations reveal burdens that Birdy is still learning to understand.
  8. Why does Birdy’s admiration for George matter?
    It expresses her desire for an idealized alternative to the future being arranged.
  9. How do changing friendships challenge her perspective?
    Others’ relationships and decisions cannot always be organized around her wishes.
  10. What does she begin to notice about others’ compromises?
    People she cares about also face constraints and make difficult choices.
  11. How does money influence the treatment of marriage?
    Economic need can turn a personal relationship into a transaction.
  12. Why is rejecting an unsuitable suitor not a complete solution?
    The household pressure continues and another arrangement can replace the rejected one.
  13. What makes the final proposed match particularly troubling?
    Birdy’s wishes and welfare are subordinated to the bargain adults have made.
  14. How does her father’s response change the conflict?
    He reconsiders treating the agreement as more important than his daughter.
  15. How does physical confrontation shape the resolution?
    The film turns the dispute into dramatic action rather than resolving it through Birdy’s objections alone.
  16. What does the ending suggest about family loyalty?
    Care can require adults to change plans and recognize the person affected by them.
  17. Which moment combines humor with a critique of power?
    Explain a specific scene and the expectation it exposes.
  18. How does modern music affect the portrayal of the past?
    It can create emotional familiarity while reminding us that this is an interpretation.
  19. How does a selected novel passage convey Birdy’s voice differently?
    Compare diary-style language with the film’s narration, performance, or imagery.
  20. What historical claim would need independent evidence?
    Select an issue involving marriage, class, or women’s opportunities rather than treating the plot as proof.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
