Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does the musical’s celebration of belonging compare with the power relationships it portrays?

  • Explain how belonging & dignity shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce musical number, representation, exploitation, and historical fiction. Use a teacher-selected reliable Barnum source. Explain that characters and relationships are substantially invented; this is not an accurate biography.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Discrimination based on race, appearance, and class; dehumanizing display themes.
  • Romantic tension, marital strain, alcohol use, fights, and a dangerous fire.
  • The celebratory narrative can obscure historical exploitation and needs explicit context.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after recruiting the performers, after the elite-party exclusion, after the concert tour strains family ties, and after the fire.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The film softens or omits documented exploitation, including Barnum’s treatment of Joice Heth. Do not reproduce dehumanizing display practices or ask students to role-play people exhibited. Compare whose voice the musical emphasizes with whose agency remains limited.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Historical context: Smithsonian · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does the musical’s celebration of belonging compare with the power relationships it portrays?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a musical number make an argument?
    Lyrics, movement, staging, and performance can promote a viewpoint or value.
  2. How does Barnum’s early experience shape the film’s ambition story?
    Class exclusion is presented as a source of his desire for recognition.
  3. What does his first business attempt reveal about spectacle?
    Attention and persuasion are central to making a display attract an audience.
  4. How does recruiting performers create a question about power?
    Barnum controls the enterprise while people displayed have their own dignity and interests.
  5. How does Phillip’s role connect entertainment with elite society?
    His status and resources offer access Barnum wants.
  6. What obstacle faces Phillip and Anne’s relationship?
    Racial and class expectations threaten their ability to act openly on their feelings.
  7. Why does Barnum pursue Jenny Lind’s tour?
    Prestigious approval appears to offer legitimacy beyond the circus’s success.
  8. What does the performers’ response assert?
    They claim dignity and solidarity rather than accepting exclusion as deserved.
  9. What does the public controversy reveal about image and conduct?
    A controlled reputation can unravel when behavior is interpreted by others.
  10. What does his later family choice suggest within the film?
    He gives greater priority to presence and relationships than constant public approval.
  11. Analyze one song’s claim about belonging or success.
    Connect its message with staging and a specific narrative conflict.
  12. What claim about Barnum should be checked historically?
    Select a depicted practice or relationship and compare it with a reliable documented source.
  13. What limitation should accompany the musical’s inspiring message?
    Its fictionalized celebration does not erase Barnum’s documented exploitation or substitute for history.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can a musical number make an argument?
    Lyrics, movement, staging, and performance can promote a viewpoint or value.
  2. Why must a historical musical be checked against other sources?
    Artistic invention and selective emphasis can reshape documented people and events.
  3. How does Barnum’s early experience shape the film’s ambition story?
    Class exclusion is presented as a source of his desire for recognition.
  4. What does his first business attempt reveal about spectacle?
    Attention and persuasion are central to making a display attract an audience.
  5. How does recruiting performers create a question about power?
    Barnum controls the enterprise while people displayed have their own dignity and interests.
  6. What does the film’s welcome promise to the performers?
    It presents a space for recognition and belonging despite external rejection.
  7. How does Phillip’s role connect entertainment with elite society?
    His status and resources offer access Barnum wants.
  8. What obstacle faces Phillip and Anne’s relationship?
    Racial and class expectations threaten their ability to act openly on their feelings.
  9. Why does Barnum pursue Jenny Lind’s tour?
    Prestigious approval appears to offer legitimacy beyond the circus’s success.
  10. How does the elite-party exclusion contradict the promise of belonging?
    Barnum distances himself from performers when their presence may threaten his social ambition.
  11. What does the performers’ response assert?
    They claim dignity and solidarity rather than accepting exclusion as deserved.
  12. How does the tour affect Barnum’s family relationships?
    Absence and pursuit of acclaim strain the commitments he claims to value.
  13. What does the public controversy reveal about image and conduct?
    A controlled reputation can unravel when behavior is interpreted by others.
  14. How does the fire change the enterprise’s immediate future?
    Destruction and danger force the group to reconsider what can continue.
  15. Why does rebuilding require more than Barnum’s ambition?
    Others’ resources, labor, and willingness to participate make continuation possible.
  16. What does his later family choice suggest within the film?
    He gives greater priority to presence and relationships than constant public approval.
  17. Analyze one song’s claim about belonging or success.
    Connect its message with staging and a specific narrative conflict.
  18. Where does the film leave performers’ agency limited?
    Identify who controls decisions, money, or representation despite inclusive language.
  19. What claim about Barnum should be checked historically?
    Select a depicted practice or relationship and compare it with a reliable documented source.
  20. What limitation should accompany the musical’s inspiring message?
    Its fictionalized celebration does not erase Barnum’s documented exploitation or substitute for history.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
