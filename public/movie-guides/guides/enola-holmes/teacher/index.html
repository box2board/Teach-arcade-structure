Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does Enola learn to choose her own path while responding to others’ needs?

  • Explain how inference shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce clue, inference, disguise, agency, and direct address. Use the 2020 first film. Supply a selected Springer passage for comparison; explain that its political intrigue is fictional.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Fights, attempted killing, gunfire, drowning danger, and injury.
  • Explosives and political violence; restrictive treatment at a school.
  • Some language and loss-related distress.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the mother’s disappearance, after the train encounter, after the finishing-school escape, and after the family mystery is resolved.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The story uses Victorian settings and debates without documenting a specific historical case. Enola’s direct address gives viewers an unusually close relationship with her account. Evaluate her mother’s secrecy rather than assuming every action is justified.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does Enola learn to choose her own path while responding to others’ needs?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What separates a clue from a conclusion?
    A clue is observed information; a conclusion interprets it and may need revision.
  2. How does Enola’s upbringing differ from others’ expectations?
    Her mother teaches skills and independence rather than conventional accomplishments alone.
  3. Why does her mother’s disappearance disrupt her life?
    It removes her closest guide and brings her brothers’ plans into conflict with her wishes.
  4. How do Sherlock and Mycroft respond differently?
    Sherlock notices her abilities while Mycroft emphasizes obedience and conventional education.
  5. How does the train encounter introduce another mystery?
    Tewkesbury is also escaping expectations and is pursued by someone dangerous.
  6. Why does Enola use disguises?
    They help her move through spaces where others would restrict or recognize her.
  7. How does London complicate her search?
    Its size, social divisions, and concealed activities create both opportunities and danger.
  8. Why does Enola question her mother’s methods?
    Discovering dangerous plans complicates her admiration and trust.
  9. What changes when she decides to help Tewkesbury?
    She takes responsibility beyond her original personal search.
  10. How does the reunion with her mother alter the search?
    It offers personal understanding without settling every question about the mother’s choices.
  11. Which choice shows independence combined with responsibility?
    Support an example such as returning to help Tewkesbury rather than only protecting herself.
  12. How would a selected novel passage change our understanding?
    Compare narration, dialogue, or an omitted detail with its screen treatment.
  13. What historical claim would need an external source?
    Select an issue such as voting reform or women’s opportunities; the fictional plot cannot establish it alone.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What separates a clue from a conclusion?
    A clue is observed information; a conclusion interprets it and may need revision.
  2. How can speaking directly to viewers shape a story?
    It creates intimacy and invites us to follow the speaker’s perspective.
  3. How does Enola’s upbringing differ from others’ expectations?
    Her mother teaches skills and independence rather than conventional accomplishments alone.
  4. Why does her mother’s disappearance disrupt her life?
    It removes her closest guide and brings her brothers’ plans into conflict with her wishes.
  5. How do Sherlock and Mycroft respond differently?
    Sherlock notices her abilities while Mycroft emphasizes obedience and conventional education.
  6. What do the hidden messages offer Enola?
    They provide resources and clues that let her begin her own investigation.
  7. How does the train encounter introduce another mystery?
    Tewkesbury is also escaping expectations and is pursued by someone dangerous.
  8. Why does Enola use disguises?
    They help her move through spaces where others would restrict or recognize her.
  9. How does London complicate her search?
    Its size, social divisions, and concealed activities create both opportunities and danger.
  10. What do her mother’s concealed materials suggest?
    They connect the disappearance with organized political activity rather than a simple accident.
  11. Why does Enola question her mother’s methods?
    Discovering dangerous plans complicates her admiration and trust.
  12. How does the finishing school restrict her agency?
    Its rules attempt to replace her independent choices with prescribed behavior.
  13. What changes when she decides to help Tewkesbury?
    She takes responsibility beyond her original personal search.
  14. How does the final threat relate to power and reform?
    Tewkesbury’s potential political role threatens someone invested in preserving the old order.
  15. Why must a detective revise an early assumption?
    The apparent explanation can conceal another motive or suspect.
  16. How does the reunion with her mother alter the search?
    It offers personal understanding without settling every question about the mother’s choices.
  17. Which choice shows independence combined with responsibility?
    Support an example such as returning to help Tewkesbury rather than only protecting herself.
  18. How does direct address influence one scene?
    Identify a specific moment and explain its effect on trust, humor, or suspense.
  19. How would a selected novel passage change our understanding?
    Compare narration, dialogue, or an omitted detail with its screen treatment.
  20. What historical claim would need an external source?
    Select an issue such as voting reform or women’s opportunities; the fictional plot cannot establish it alone.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
