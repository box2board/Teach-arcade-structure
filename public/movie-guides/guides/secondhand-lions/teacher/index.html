Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What can a story offer when its factual details remain uncertain?

  • Explain how trust shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce frame narrative, unreliable account, evidence, and trust. Preview abuse and the lion’s protective attack. Analyze characters without asking students to explain their own family circumstances.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Neglect, manipulation, and physical abuse of a child.
  • Fights, threats, gunfire, a lion attack, and animal and human deaths.
  • Smoking, drinking, language, and stereotyped adventure depictions.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after Walter is left with the uncles, after an adventure account, after the attempted theft and assault, and after the final visitor.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The uncles’ adventures are presented as stories within a fictional film. Distinguish believing a value from verifying an event. Exoticized adventure imagery may simplify places and people; discuss those limits rather than treating it as historical evidence.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

AFI film credits · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What can a story offer when its factual details remain uncertain?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How is trusting a person different from verifying every story they tell?
    Trust concerns conduct and relationships; factual verification requires independent evidence.
  2. Why is Walter left with the uncles?
    His mother places him there while pursuing her own plans and interest in their money.
  3. How do the uncles initially respond to his presence?
    They are guarded and unfamiliar as caregivers, making the arrangement uncertain.
  4. Why does Walter wonder about their wealth?
    Its unexplained origin invites competing stories and affects others’ motives.
  5. What does the bar confrontation reveal about Hub?
    It shows force and a strong self-image, but physical prowess alone does not establish wisdom.
  6. Why does purchasing the lion change the household routine?
    An aging animal becomes part of their unusual shared life rather than simply a hunting target.
  7. How does Walter’s relationship with the lion develop?
    Care and time turn the animal into a valued presence.
  8. How does Hub’s account of values differ from a factual claim?
    It presents principles he wants to live by rather than evidence proving an adventure.
  9. What does the assault reveal about the difference between words and conduct?
    Claims of care are contradicted by abusive behavior.
  10. What does the later visitor add to the adventure accounts?
    The connection provides support for parts of the story without independently verifying every detail.
  11. Which ordinary action best demonstrates care?
    Identify practical attention, protection, or shared work and explain its effect.
  12. What portrayal in an adventure flashback deserves questioning?
    Identify an exoticized or simplified depiction and explain what perspective is missing.
  13. What is a careful conclusion about Walter’s decision to trust?
    Base it on observed conduct and acknowledge that trust does not require accepting every claim uncritically.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How is trusting a person different from verifying every story they tell?
    Trust concerns conduct and relationships; factual verification requires independent evidence.
  2. What can a story communicate beyond whether each event occurred?
    It can express values, hopes, identity, or a way of interpreting experience.
  3. Why is Walter left with the uncles?
    His mother places him there while pursuing her own plans and interest in their money.
  4. How do the uncles initially respond to his presence?
    They are guarded and unfamiliar as caregivers, making the arrangement uncertain.
  5. Why does Walter wonder about their wealth?
    Its unexplained origin invites competing stories and affects others’ motives.
  6. How do the adventure accounts change his view of them?
    They make their past seem larger while leaving questions about truth.
  7. What does the bar confrontation reveal about Hub?
    It shows force and a strong self-image, but physical prowess alone does not establish wisdom.
  8. Why does purchasing the lion change the household routine?
    An aging animal becomes part of their unusual shared life rather than simply a hunting target.
  9. How does Walter’s relationship with the lion develop?
    Care and time turn the animal into a valued presence.
  10. What do gardening and shared tasks add to the relationship?
    Ordinary activities create connection beyond spectacular stories.
  11. How does Hub’s account of values differ from a factual claim?
    It presents principles he wants to live by rather than evidence proving an adventure.
  12. Why does Walter’s mother’s return threaten the growing trust?
    Her plans and companion’s motives place his welfare and the uncles’ money at risk.
  13. What does the assault reveal about the difference between words and conduct?
    Claims of care are contradicted by abusive behavior.
  14. How does the lion’s intervention affect the conflict?
    It protects Walter and makes the loss of the animal part of the story’s emotional cost.
  15. Why does Walter choose to remain with the uncles?
    Their developing care offers a more trustworthy relationship than the alternative he faces.
  16. What does the later visitor add to the adventure accounts?
    The connection provides support for parts of the story without independently verifying every detail.
  17. Which ordinary action best demonstrates care?
    Identify practical attention, protection, or shared work and explain its effect.
  18. How does the film distinguish a meaningful story from proven history?
    Its framing and later clues invite belief while leaving limits to verification.
  19. What portrayal in an adventure flashback deserves questioning?
    Identify an exoticized or simplified depiction and explain what perspective is missing.
  20. What is a careful conclusion about Walter’s decision to trust?
    Base it on observed conduct and acknowledge that trust does not require accepting every claim uncritically.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
