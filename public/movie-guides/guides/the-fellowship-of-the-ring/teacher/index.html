Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What makes a person trustworthy when power offers an easier path?

  • Explain how power & temptation shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Use the approximately 178-minute theatrical edition; the extended edition is about 208 minutes and adds scenes. Introduce quest, motif, exposition, and temptation. This is the first film in a larger story, so its ending intentionally leaves the main quest unfinished.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Frequent fantasy combat, arrows, stabbing, decapitation or injury detail, and deaths.
  • Frightening monsters, pursuit, dark environments, and intense peril.
  • Smoking and drinking; disturbing transformation and temptation scenes.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the departure from the Shire, after the Council of Elrond, after Moria, and when the Fellowship separates.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

Compare a teacher-selected Tolkien passage with its film scene. The adaptation omits episodes such as Tom Bombadil and moves some events across book boundaries; Boromir’s death appears in the next book. Discuss fantasy symbols as interpretations rather than forcing a single allegory.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What makes a person trustworthy when power offers an easier path?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. Why might power tempt someone who intends to do good?
    It can promise quick solutions while changing how that person treats others.
  2. Why does Gandalf investigate Bilbo’s ring?
    He suspects that it is the dangerous One Ring rather than an ordinary possession.
  3. Why must Frodo leave the Shire?
    Remaining there would expose him and the community to those seeking the Ring.
  4. How does Sam’s decision to accompany Frodo establish loyalty?
    He accepts risk and supports a friend beyond familiar surroundings.
  5. How does Aragorn help the travelers before Rivendell?
    He guides and protects them while they learn to judge his character.
  6. What does Frodo’s injury reveal about the quest’s danger?
    The threat can harm him directly and requires help beyond his own abilities.
  7. Why can the Ring not simply be kept or used safely by the group?
    Its corrupting power makes possession and intended use dangerous.
  8. What does the Fellowship’s mixed membership make possible?
    Different skills and perspectives support a task no member can complete alone.
  9. How does Gandalf’s fall affect the group?
    They lose a guide and must continue through grief and uncertainty.
  10. Why does Frodo leave, and why does Sam follow?
    Frodo fears further corruption of companions; Sam refuses to let him carry the burden alone.
  11. Choose a moment when restraint shows strength.
    Accept refusal of the Ring or another supported example of resisting an easy use of power.
  12. Why is the ending not a complete resolution of the larger conflict?
    The quest continues after the Fellowship separates; this film is one part of the story.
  13. What effect might removing a novel episode have on the adaptation?
    It can change pace, world-building, or themes; explain with a teacher-selected comparison.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. Why might power tempt someone who intends to do good?
    It can promise quick solutions while changing how that person treats others.
  2. What makes a quest different from a simple journey?
    A difficult purpose, obstacles, and changing responsibilities shape the traveler.
  3. Why does Gandalf investigate Bilbo’s ring?
    He suspects that it is the dangerous One Ring rather than an ordinary possession.
  4. Why must Frodo leave the Shire?
    Remaining there would expose him and the community to those seeking the Ring.
  5. How does Sam’s decision to accompany Frodo establish loyalty?
    He accepts risk and supports a friend beyond familiar surroundings.
  6. What makes the Black Riders threatening?
    They pursue the Ring and create danger even when the hobbits try to hide.
  7. How does Aragorn help the travelers before Rivendell?
    He guides and protects them while they learn to judge his character.
  8. What does Frodo’s injury reveal about the quest’s danger?
    The threat can harm him directly and requires help beyond his own abilities.
  9. Why can the Ring not simply be kept or used safely by the group?
    Its corrupting power makes possession and intended use dangerous.
  10. Why does Frodo volunteer at the council?
    He accepts responsibility when others disagree about how to carry the burden.
  11. What does the Fellowship’s mixed membership make possible?
    Different skills and perspectives support a task no member can complete alone.
  12. Why do the companions enter Moria?
    Their route options narrow, leading them to a dangerous passage through the mountains.
  13. How does Gandalf’s fall affect the group?
    They lose a guide and must continue through grief and uncertainty.
  14. How does Galadriel’s response illustrate temptation?
    She recognizes the promise of power and ultimately refuses to take the Ring.
  15. Why does Boromir try to take the Ring?
    He believes it could help his people, but desire for its power overwhelms his restraint.
  16. Why does Frodo leave, and why does Sam follow?
    Frodo fears further corruption of companions; Sam refuses to let him carry the burden alone.
  17. Choose a moment when restraint shows strength.
    Accept refusal of the Ring or another supported example of resisting an easy use of power.
  18. How does the film make a small protagonist seem important?
    Accept contrasts of scale, framing, attention to choices, or companions’ responses.
  19. Why is the ending not a complete resolution of the larger conflict?
    The quest continues after the Fellowship separates; this film is one part of the story.
  20. What effect might removing a novel episode have on the adaptation?
    It can change pace, world-building, or themes; explain with a teacher-selected comparison.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
