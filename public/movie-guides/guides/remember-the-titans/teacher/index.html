Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What changes a group from sharing a goal to taking responsibility for one another?

  • Analyze actions that build or damage trust.
  • Compare different forms of player and coach leadership.
  • Distinguish the team’s story from the wider desegregation process.

Introduce desegregation, discrimination, accountability, and cooperation. Locate Alexandria and distinguish the 1971 consolidation from the start of all desegregation there. Students need no detailed football knowledge; explain only what a scene requires. Discuss stereotypes without repeating slurs.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Racist hostility, derogatory language, fights, and football collisions.
  • A serious car crash, paralysis, grief, and funeral scenes.
  • Some profanity, brief kissing, and sexual references.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the camp assignments, after the players return home, after conflicts over loyalty and fair play, and after the injury and championship.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The film dramatizes relationships, conflicts, and game events. Alexandria’s school history shows a longer struggle and earlier desegregation steps, not a city that first encountered integration in one football season. Pair one film claim with the city or school district history. Team success is not evidence that racial inequality ended.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What changes a group from sharing a goal to taking responsibility for one another?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What is the difference between being included in a group and feeling that you belong?
    Access alone does not guarantee respect, trust, or meaningful participation.
  2. Why does Boone’s appointment create tension?
    He replaces Yoast as head coach amid school consolidation and racial division, challenging existing expectations.
  3. How does Boone use room and bus assignments at camp?
    He requires cross-racial contact rather than allowing players to remain in familiar separate groups.
  4. What do the players initially resist at camp?
    They resist learning about teammates across racial lines and working within one shared team.
  5. What message does the Gettysburg visit add to camp?
    It places their hostility against a history of destructive division and urges a different relationship.
  6. What changes between Gerry and Julius as they work together?
    They develop respect and friendship through honest confrontation and shared effort.
  7. Why is returning home different from succeeding at camp?
    Family, friends, and community divisions still pressure the players outside the team environment.
  8. What does public resistance reveal about the limits of one team’s progress?
    A stronger team relationship does not automatically change every community attitude or institution.
  9. How does Gerry’s injury affect the group?
    It creates distress and requires teammates to support him beyond the football field.
  10. What does the later funeral framing suggest about the team’s bonds?
    The relationships endure beyond one season, while remembrance includes loss as well as achievement.
  11. Choose a moment when someone gives up an advantage to act responsibly.
    Accept an accurate player or coach choice with explanation of the cost and its effect on trust.
  12. Why would it be incorrect to say a winning season ended racism in Alexandria?
    A sports outcome cannot establish the end of wider systemic inequality; the historical process is longer.
  13. What would you check using Alexandria’s school-history sources?
    A specific date, consolidation claim, or desegregation milestone, distinguishing it from dramatized dialogue and conflict.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What is the difference between being included in a group and feeling that you belong?
    Access alone does not guarantee respect, trust, or meaningful participation.
  2. How can accountability strengthen a team?
    Members follow shared expectations, acknowledge harm, and address actions that undermine others.
  3. Why does Boone’s appointment create tension?
    He replaces Yoast as head coach amid school consolidation and racial division, challenging existing expectations.
  4. How does Boone use room and bus assignments at camp?
    He requires cross-racial contact rather than allowing players to remain in familiar separate groups.
  5. What do the players initially resist at camp?
    They resist learning about teammates across racial lines and working within one shared team.
  6. How does Julius challenge Gerry’s claim to leadership?
    He points to unequal commitment and Gerry’s failure to hold teammates accountable for poor treatment and play.
  7. What message does the Gettysburg visit add to camp?
    It places their hostility against a history of destructive division and urges a different relationship.
  8. What changes between Gerry and Julius as they work together?
    They develop respect and friendship through honest confrontation and shared effort.
  9. Why is returning home different from succeeding at camp?
    Family, friends, and community divisions still pressure the players outside the team environment.
  10. How does Gerry respond when a teammate deliberately undermines cooperation?
    He supports removing the player rather than protecting an old friendship at the team’s expense.
  11. What does public resistance reveal about the limits of one team’s progress?
    A stronger team relationship does not automatically change every community attitude or institution.
  12. How does Yoast’s response to unfair officiating portray responsibility?
    He challenges treatment he sees as unjust even when doing so threatens his own interests in the film.
  13. How does Gerry’s injury affect the group?
    It creates distress and requires teammates to support him beyond the football field.
  14. What does Julius’s hospital visit show about their relationship?
    He acts as a close friend and chosen brother, demonstrating belonging beyond initial divisions.
  15. How does the team respond to the challenge of playing without Gerry?
    Players adapt and take responsibility for supporting one another toward their shared goal.
  16. What does the later funeral framing suggest about the team’s bonds?
    The relationships endure beyond one season, while remembrance includes loss as well as achievement.
  17. Choose a moment when someone gives up an advantage to act responsibly.
    Accept an accurate player or coach choice with explanation of the cost and its effect on trust.
  18. What is the difference between Boone’s strict rules and the players’ own leadership?
    Rules create contact and expectations; players must choose to build trust and confront harmful conduct themselves.
  19. Why would it be incorrect to say a winning season ended racism in Alexandria?
    A sports outcome cannot establish the end of wider systemic inequality; the historical process is longer.
  20. What would you check using Alexandria’s school-history sources?
    A specific date, consolidation claim, or desegregation milestone, distinguishing it from dramatized dialogue and conflict.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
