Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How can words serve both control and human connection?

  • Explain how power of language shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce propaganda, censorship, narrator, and persecution. Provide age-appropriate historical context and a selected Zusak passage. Follow responsible Holocaust-teaching practices; avoid simulations or comparisons of suffering. The studio lists about 129 minutes; other releases may vary.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Nazi persecution, antisemitism, public humiliation, and threats.
  • Deaths, bombing, injury, family separation, and intense grief.
  • Discriminatory language and political imagery; do not require students to repeat slurs.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the first reading lessons, after the book burning, after Max arrives, and before the bombing aftermath.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The story is historical fiction with Death as a literary narrator. German civilian experiences do not represent the full history of the Holocaust. Pair the portrayal of persecution with a teacher-selected USHMM source that centers documented Jewish experiences.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How can words serve both control and human connection?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can language be used to control people?
    It can spread propaganda, define who belongs, or restrict which stories are heard.
  2. How does Liesel’s arrival establish loss and uncertainty?
    Separation and her brother’s death leave her entering an unfamiliar household.
  3. How does Hans begin to build trust with her?
    Patience, care, and reading lessons create a relationship beyond formal obligation.
  4. Why does learning to read matter to Liesel?
    It gives her access to stories, expression, and a sense of agency.
  5. What does the book-burning event reveal about power?
    Public destruction of books demonstrates control over ideas and enforced conformity.
  6. Why does Liesel take a book from the aftermath?
    Her attachment to words conflicts with the approved public action.
  7. Why does Max need to hide?
    As a Jewish man, he is targeted by Nazi persecution.
  8. How do Liesel and Max connect through words?
    Descriptions, stories, and conversation help sustain a relationship during confinement.
  9. Why does Hans’s public gesture of help create danger?
    An act of care challenges the regime’s treatment of persecuted people.
  10. How does the bombing aftermath change the story?
    Deaths transform familiar relationships into memories Liesel must carry.
  11. Which scene shows words serving connection rather than control?
    Explain a specific act of reading, describing, or storytelling and its effect.
  12. How does a selected novel passage differ from the film?
    Compare narration, imagery, or withheld information with evidence from both forms.
  13. What historical question requires evidence beyond this story?
    Name a question about Nazi persecution and identify an appropriate documented source.

    An act of care challenges the regime’s treatment of persecuted people.
  14. How does Max’s departure affect Liesel?
    It intensifies uncertainty and shows how persecution can disrupt connection.
  15. What does the air-raid shelter sequence suggest about storytelling?
    Words can provide temporary shared focus and comfort during fear.
  16. How does the bombing aftermath change the story?
    Deaths transform familiar relationships into memories Liesel must carry.

    Explain a specific act of reading, describing, or storytelling and its effect.
  18. How does Death’s narration shape one moment?
    Identify a line or narrative position without quoting extensively and explain its effect.

    Compare narration, imagery, or withheld information with evidence from both forms.
  20. What historical question requires evidence beyond this story?
    Name a question about Nazi persecution and identify an appropriate documented source.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
