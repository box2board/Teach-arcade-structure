Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does the film show a person’s value through relationships and choices?

  • Explain how community & interdependence shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Preview the crisis near the bridge. Offer an alternative without requiring students to explain why. Discuss character actions, not students’ personal crises. Introduce counterfactual narrative and community institutions.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Suicidal thoughts and a crisis at a bridge; intense despair and threatened self-harm.
  • A child’s death is discussed in an alternate reality; violence, bullying, and injury.
  • Alcohol, family conflict, and dated portrayals of gender and race.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after George’s delayed plans, after the bank-run sequence, before the bridge crisis, and after the alternate-town sequence.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The fantasy offers one dramatic response to despair, not a model of mental-health care or a claim that gratitude alone resolves crisis. Treat the Building and Loan as a narrative institution within its historical setting, not financial instruction. The film predates the modern MPAA rating system.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Studio film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does the film show a person’s value through relationships and choices?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What can a counterfactual story help a viewer examine?
    Imagining a different outcome can highlight causes, relationships, or values in the original world.
  2. What does George’s childhood rescue of Harry establish?
    It shows care and personal cost early in his story.
  3. How do George’s travel ambitions shape our understanding of him?
    They reveal a desired life beyond Bedford Falls, making later sacrifices more visible.
  4. Why does he remain involved with the Building and Loan?
    Family responsibilities and the institution’s role in the community repeatedly influence his choices.
  5. How does George’s relationship with Mary complicate the idea of sacrifice?
    Their shared life brings connection as well as responsibilities and altered plans.
  6. What happens during the bank-run sequence?
    George explains the institution’s situation and asks neighbors to accept limited withdrawals.
  7. How does Mary’s contribution affect that crisis?
    Their available honeymoon money helps meet immediate needs and keep the institution open.
  8. Why does the missing deposit become a crisis for George?
    The loss threatens the institution and creates financial and legal pressure he feels responsible for.
  9. How does George’s distress affect his interactions at home?
    Pressure spills into anger and disconnection, showing harm to relationships he values.
  10. What do the neighbors’ actions at the end demonstrate?
    They respond with practical collective help and recognition of relationships built over time.
  11. Which ordinary action has a large effect in the film’s argument?
    Explain a supported example and trace its effect on another person or the community.
  12. What limitation should accompany discussion of the hopeful ending?
    A fictional resolution does not show that severe distress or structural pressures are always solved so quickly.
  13. How does one repeated image, sound, or setting reinforce community?
    Identify a specific element and connect its recurrence to relationships or perspective.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What can a counterfactual story help a viewer examine?
    Imagining a different outcome can highlight causes, relationships, or values in the original world.
  2. Why might a person’s contribution be difficult to recognize?
    Its effects may be dispersed across everyday relationships rather than measured by one achievement.
  3. What does George’s childhood rescue of Harry establish?
    It shows care and personal cost early in his story.
  4. How do George’s travel ambitions shape our understanding of him?
    They reveal a desired life beyond Bedford Falls, making later sacrifices more visible.
  5. Why does he remain involved with the Building and Loan?
    Family responsibilities and the institution’s role in the community repeatedly influence his choices.
  6. How does Potter’s view of the town differ from George’s?
    Potter emphasizes control and profit while George emphasizes access and neighbors’ needs.
  7. How does George’s relationship with Mary complicate the idea of sacrifice?
    Their shared life brings connection as well as responsibilities and altered plans.
  8. What happens during the bank-run sequence?
    George explains the institution’s situation and asks neighbors to accept limited withdrawals.
  9. How does Mary’s contribution affect that crisis?
    Their available honeymoon money helps meet immediate needs and keep the institution open.
  10. What does the development of homes suggest about community opportunity?
    The film connects access to housing with dignity, independence, and shared growth.
  11. Why does the missing deposit become a crisis for George?
    The loss threatens the institution and creates financial and legal pressure he feels responsible for.
  12. How does Potter exploit the situation?
    He retains the money he finds and uses George’s vulnerability rather than helping resolve the loss.
  13. How does George’s distress affect his interactions at home?
    Pressure spills into anger and disconnection, showing harm to relationships he values.
  14. What does the alternate Bedford Falls allow him to notice?
    It imagines how relationships and events might differ without his actions.
  15. Why is the alternate world an argument rather than evidence from real history?
    It is a constructed fantasy designed to emphasize the film’s interpretation of George’s importance.
  16. What do the neighbors’ actions at the end demonstrate?
    They respond with practical collective help and recognition of relationships built over time.
  17. Which ordinary action has a large effect in the film’s argument?
    Explain a supported example and trace its effect on another person or the community.
  18. How does the film complicate success as wealth or travel?
    It presents relationships and shared contributions alongside ambitions that remain unfulfilled.
  19. What limitation should accompany discussion of the hopeful ending?
    A fictional resolution does not show that severe distress or structural pressures are always solved so quickly.
  20. How does one repeated image, sound, or setting reinforce community?
    Identify a specific element and connect its recurrence to relationships or perspective.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
