Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How can listening to other people change a person’s definition of success?

  • Explain how mentorship & listening shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Released in Los Angeles in December 1995 and broadly in January 1996. Introduce composition, mentorship, accessibility, and perspective. Discuss Cole’s deafness respectfully: it is not a failure to be repaired, and no student should disclose a disability.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Arguments about disability, parental frustration, and inaccessible communication.
  • A former student’s wartime death and grief.
  • Romantic tension between an adult teacher and a student; some language and smoking.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the early teaching difficulties, after Cole’s deafness is identified, during their later conflict, and at the final concert.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The story is fictional and compresses three decades. Holland’s difficulty connecting with Cole reflects the father’s limitations, not an absence of value in Cole’s life. Analyze opportunities for accessible communication and music without treating the film as representative of all Deaf experience. Teacher-selected firsthand perspectives can broaden the comparison.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How can listening to other people change a person’s definition of success?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How might achievement include effects on others as well as a personal product?
    Mentorship, relationships, and shared opportunities can matter alongside an individual goal.
  2. Why does Holland initially accept the teaching job?
    He needs stable work while hoping to compose in his remaining time.
  3. What makes his first teaching approach difficult for students?
    He initially assumes knowledge and interest they do not yet have.
  4. How does connecting lessons with familiar music change participation?
    It gives students an entry point and makes ideas more meaningful to them.
  5. How does work begin to affect his composing goal?
    Teaching and family responsibilities consume time and change his priorities.
  6. What changes when the family learns Cole is deaf?
    They must consider new forms of communication and challenge their assumptions.
  7. How does Iris respond to Cole’s communication needs?
    She actively learns and supports communication rather than treating him as unable to connect.
  8. How does a former student’s wartime death affect the school community?
    It brings grief and connects classroom relationships with events beyond school.
  9. Why does Holland’s interaction with Rowena require boundaries?
    An adult teacher’s admiration and emotional involvement must not displace professional responsibility.
  10. Why do former students organize the final concert?
    They honor the teacher’s influence and perform the composition he thought unfinished as a public achievement.
  11. Choose a teaching change that responds to a learner’s needs.
    Accept a specific adjustment in instruction, confidence-building, rhythm, or accessibility.
  12. Why is Holland’s legacy broader than completing a symphony?
    His relationships and students’ lives show lasting effects beyond one composition.
  13. What perspective could expand or challenge the film’s portrayal of deafness?
    A teacher-selected firsthand Deaf account can show experiences and values the film does not include.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How might achievement include effects on others as well as a personal product?
    Mentorship, relationships, and shared opportunities can matter alongside an individual goal.
  2. What does accessible communication require beyond good intentions?
    It requires attention to the other person’s language, needs, and ability to participate.
  3. Why does Holland initially accept the teaching job?
    He needs stable work while hoping to compose in his remaining time.
  4. What makes his first teaching approach difficult for students?
    He initially assumes knowledge and interest they do not yet have.
  5. How does connecting lessons with familiar music change participation?
    It gives students an entry point and makes ideas more meaningful to them.
  6. How does Holland help Gertrude Lang with her clarinet playing?
    He looks beyond mistakes toward confidence and a more useful way to approach the music.
  7. How does work begin to affect his composing goal?
    Teaching and family responsibilities consume time and change his priorities.
  8. What changes when the family learns Cole is deaf?
    They must consider new forms of communication and challenge their assumptions.
  9. How does Iris respond to Cole’s communication needs?
    She actively learns and supports communication rather than treating him as unable to connect.
  10. Why does the work with a struggling drummer matter?
    Holland and colleagues adapt instruction and provide persistent support.
  11. How does a former student’s wartime death affect the school community?
    It brings grief and connects classroom relationships with events beyond school.
  12. What problem does Cole identify in his relationship with his father?
    Holland gives students attention while often failing to listen to or include his son.
  13. Why does Holland’s interaction with Rowena require boundaries?
    An adult teacher’s admiration and emotional involvement must not displace professional responsibility.
  14. What choice does Holland make about leaving with Rowena?
    He remains with his family and responsibilities rather than pursuing that departure.
  15. How does Holland’s later musical event attempt to include Cole?
    He combines visual and signed communication with music, changing how the experience is shared.
  16. Why do former students organize the final concert?
    They honor the teacher’s influence and perform the composition he thought unfinished as a public achievement.
  17. Choose a teaching change that responds to a learner’s needs.
    Accept a specific adjustment in instruction, confidence-building, rhythm, or accessibility.
  18. How does Cole challenge Holland’s idea of what matters?
    He asks for attention and connection rather than allowing work to excuse absence.
  19. Why is Holland’s legacy broader than completing a symphony?
    His relationships and students’ lives show lasting effects beyond one composition.
  20. What perspective could expand or challenge the film’s portrayal of deafness?
    A teacher-selected firsthand Deaf account can show experiences and values the film does not include.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
