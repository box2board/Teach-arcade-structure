Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does Schindler’s List portray changes in responsibility under a system of persecution?

  • Track character choices and the pressures shaping them.
  • Support an interpretation with accurately described scenes.
  • Distinguish film portrayal from historical evidence and identify a source to investigate.

Establish a factual Holocaust timeline before viewing: Nazi antisemitism, invasion and occupation, ghettos, deportation, forced labor, and genocide. Clarify differences among ghettos, concentration camps, and killing centers. Introduce Kraków, Płaszów, Auschwitz, and Brünnlitz. Emphasize that rescue was exceptional and the Holocaust cannot be explained through one rescuer’s story.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Graphic executions, killings, persecution of children, bodies, and sustained genocidal violence.
  • Nudity during dehumanizing inspections and sexual situations; sexual coercion or assault.
  • Strong language, antisemitic abuse, terror, separation, and trauma. Preview the entire assigned edition.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Do not require personal disclosures or reenactments of persecution.

Pause after factory recruitment, after the ghetto liquidation, during the move toward the list and relocation, and after liberation and the memorial epilogue. At roughly 195 minutes this needs a multi-period plan. Check transitions in your edition and bridge omitted scenes with factual context.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Follow USHMM teaching guidance: provide context, avoid simulations and comparisons of suffering, and choose images with care. Do not ask students to role-play victims or perpetrators. Offer a document-based route to the same learning goals.

Historical context & source comparison

This dramatization is not archival footage even where it uses black and white. Read the USHMM Schindler overview and a teacher-selected survivor testimony, asking what each adds to or complicates in the film. Recognize Jewish people as individuals with histories, relationships, and agency; also consider Emilie Schindler’s contribution. Do not reduce the Holocaust to an inspiring ending or imply that those not rescued lacked courage.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What changes in Schindler’s priorities, and what evidence supports that interpretation?
  • How do Stern and other Jewish characters make choices under severe constraints?
  • How does a rescue story illuminate history while leaving much outside its frame?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one historical question.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. Why must a story about one rescue effort be placed within the wider Holocaust?
    It concerns exceptional circumstances and cannot represent all victims, communities, places, or the scale and mechanisms of genocide.
  2. What initially draws Schindler to wartime business in Kraków?
    He seeks profit and status through wartime opportunities and connections rather than beginning as a committed rescuer.
  3. What important roles does Itzhak Stern play?
    He contributes business expertise, organizes workers, and helps preserve lives through decisions about employment and documentation.
  4. Why can being classified as an essential worker matter in the film?
    Work status can offer limited protection from deportation or death, though that protection remains unstable under Nazi power.
  5. What changes for Jewish residents during the ghetto liquidation?
    Forced removal and violence destroy remaining security and separate or displace people. No graphic account is needed.
  6. How does witnessing persecution affect Schindler’s outlook?
    The suffering becomes harder for him to treat as distant business context; later protective actions suggest changing priorities.
  7. How does the film contrast Göth’s power with the workers’ vulnerability?
    Arbitrary threats and violence show domination over people with few protections. Explain the imbalance without recounting graphic acts.
  8. What risks accompany Stern’s efforts to protect workers?
    He acts under coercive authority where paperwork, selections, and mistakes can have deadly consequences; his choices are constrained.
  9. What is the purpose of the list in the film’s rescue effort?
    It identifies people for transfer to Schindler’s factory at Brünnlitz, creating a route toward protection from further persecution.
  10. What does Schindler give up as his priorities change?
    He spends money and risks status and safety to secure and support workers rather than maximize profit.
  11. What does the liberation sequence change for the workers?
    Nazi control ends as the war concludes, but loss, displacement, and rebuilding remain. Survival is not restoration of everything lost.
  12. Choose a scene showing Jewish agency rather than only Schindler’s actions. Explain it.
    Accept a specific decision by Stern or another Jewish character, with explanation of both action and constraints.
  13. What question would you investigate using survivor testimony or a historical document?
    Choose a specific question, an appropriate source, and what it may reveal or omit; do not treat one testimony as every person’s experience.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. Why must a story about one rescue effort be placed within the wider Holocaust?
    It concerns exceptional circumstances and cannot represent all victims, communities, places, or the scale and mechanisms of genocide.
  2. What makes a dramatized scene different from archival evidence?
    Actors, scripted dialogue, staging, and editing reconstruct a scene; historical claims require independent sources.
  3. What initially draws Schindler to wartime business in Kraków?
    He seeks profit and status through wartime opportunities and connections rather than beginning as a committed rescuer.
  4. How do Schindler’s relationships with Nazi officials benefit his factory?
    Social connections, gifts, and influence secure business advantages and later become tools for protecting workers.
  5. What important roles does Itzhak Stern play?
    He contributes business expertise, organizes workers, and helps preserve lives through decisions about employment and documentation.
  6. Why can being classified as an essential worker matter in the film?
    Work status can offer limited protection from deportation or death, though that protection remains unstable under Nazi power.
  7. How does the film show Jewish people helping one another under restrictions?
    Accept a specific example of information sharing, family support, organizing employment, or assistance, recognizing constrained agency.
  8. What changes for Jewish residents during the ghetto liquidation?
    Forced removal and violence destroy remaining security and separate or displace people. No graphic account is needed.
  9. How does witnessing persecution affect Schindler’s outlook?
    The suffering becomes harder for him to treat as distant business context; later protective actions suggest changing priorities.
  10. How does the film contrast Göth’s power with the workers’ vulnerability?
    Arbitrary threats and violence show domination over people with few protections. Explain the imbalance without recounting graphic acts.
  11. What risks accompany Stern’s efforts to protect workers?
    He acts under coercive authority where paperwork, selections, and mistakes can have deadly consequences; his choices are constrained.
  12. Why does protection based only on one official’s favor remain uncertain?
    Orders, transfers, and arbitrary decisions can overturn it; a personal exception does not dismantle the persecuting system.
  13. What is the purpose of the list in the film’s rescue effort?
    It identifies people for transfer to Schindler’s factory at Brünnlitz, creating a route toward protection from further persecution.
  14. What does Schindler give up as his priorities change?
    He spends money and risks status and safety to secure and support workers rather than maximize profit.
  15. Why does relocation not immediately remove every danger?
    People still face transport risks, separation, and Nazi control; the film shows rescue as precarious rather than automatic.
  16. What does the liberation sequence change for the workers?
    Nazi control ends as the war concludes, but loss, displacement, and rebuilding remain. Survival is not restoration of everything lost.
  17. Choose a scene showing Jewish agency rather than only Schindler’s actions. Explain it.
    Accept a specific decision by Stern or another Jewish character, with explanation of both action and constraints.
  18. How does a visual choice affect your interpretation of a scene?
    Accept analysis of framing, black and white, selective color, or contrast. Symbols can support more than one reasoned interpretation.
  19. What does the memorial epilogue add to the dramatized story?
    It connects portrayed events to survivors, remembrance, and real lives, while the main film remains a constructed narrative.
  20. What question would you investigate using survivor testimony or a historical document?
    Choose a specific question, an appropriate source, and what it may reveal or omit; do not treat one testimony as every person’s experience.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of film as historical evidence. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
