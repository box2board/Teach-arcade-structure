Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What does the coach need to understand before he can support this team well?

  • Explain how teamwork shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce cross-country scoring, community, assumption, and opportunity. Center students and families as contributors. Use a teacher-selected local or historical source to check a claim about the real team.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Family hardship, threats, fights, and a teen’s distress near a bridge.
  • An assault injures a girl; discussion of imprisonment and violence.
  • Some language and demanding agricultural work.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the first training effort, after White joins work in the fields, after a family conflict over participation, and before the state meet.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The dramatization reshapes events and relationships for a sports narrative. Avoid treating the coach as the sole source of change or agricultural work as a simple athletic advantage. Discuss the costs and knowledge involved in the runners’ daily lives.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What does the coach need to understand before he can support this team well?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. Why should a coach question assumptions about an unfamiliar community?
    Local knowledge and circumstances can make initial judgments incomplete or inaccurate.
  2. Why does Jim White arrive in McFarland?
    A professional setback leads him to seek work and move his family.
  3. How does his family initially respond to the town?
    Unfamiliar surroundings create discomfort before relationships and understanding develop.
  4. What does White notice about the students’ running?
    Their speed and endurance suggest potential for a cross-country team.
  5. How do jobs and family needs complicate practice?
    Time and energy are already committed to important responsibilities.
  6. What does an early race reveal about preparation?
    Ability alone does not replace strategy and experience with a course.
  7. How does White’s experience in the fields change his understanding?
    It makes the physical demands and family obligations more concrete to him.
  8. How does negotiating with families affect participation?
    Understanding their needs helps create arrangements rather than demanding unquestioned attendance.
  9. How does community support become visible?
    Food, events, encouragement, and practical help show a shared investment in the runners.
  10. What does White’s final choice suggest about belonging?
    He recognizes relationships and community value beyond an apparently more prestigious position.
  11. Which scene shows a revised assumption?
    Identify what White or another character first believed and what new experience changed it.
  12. How does a film technique shape a race scene?
    Explain a specific use of editing, sound, framing, or reactions.
  13. What claim about the real team would require an outside source?
    Select a depicted event and distinguish the film’s version from independent evidence.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. Why should a coach question assumptions about an unfamiliar community?
    Local knowledge and circumstances can make initial judgments incomplete or inaccurate.
  2. How does a team result differ from one star’s performance?
    The contributions of several members combine to determine the outcome.
  3. Why does Jim White arrive in McFarland?
    A professional setback leads him to seek work and move his family.
  4. How does his family initially respond to the town?
    Unfamiliar surroundings create discomfort before relationships and understanding develop.
  5. What does White notice about the students’ running?
    Their speed and endurance suggest potential for a cross-country team.
  6. Why is forming a team more than selecting fast runners?
    Participation requires training, trust, schedules, and support.
  7. How do jobs and family needs complicate practice?
    Time and energy are already committed to important responsibilities.
  8. What does an early race reveal about preparation?
    Ability alone does not replace strategy and experience with a course.
  9. How does White’s experience in the fields change his understanding?
    It makes the physical demands and family obligations more concrete to him.
  10. Why does missing a family event matter to his relationships?
    Commitment to coaching can create costs for his own household if communication fails.
  11. How does negotiating with families affect participation?
    Understanding their needs helps create arrangements rather than demanding unquestioned attendance.
  12. What pressures does Thomas face beyond racing?
    Home circumstances and distress complicate the simple image of an athlete pursuing success.
  13. How does community support become visible?
    Food, events, encouragement, and practical help show a shared investment in the runners.
  14. Why should Danny’s contribution be evaluated within team scoring?
    A runner who is not the individual winner can still materially affect the collective result.
  15. How does the state meet bring earlier learning together?
    Preparation, persistence, and members’ contributions become a shared performance.
  16. What does White’s final choice suggest about belonging?
    He recognizes relationships and community value beyond an apparently more prestigious position.
  17. Which scene shows a revised assumption?
    Identify what White or another character first believed and what new experience changed it.
  18. What does the film reveal about opportunity beyond motivation?
    Resources, time, family agreement, and institutional access affect participation.
  19. How does a film technique shape a race scene?
    Explain a specific use of editing, sound, framing, or reactions.
  20. What claim about the real team would require an outside source?
    Select a depicted event and distinguish the film’s version from independent evidence.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
