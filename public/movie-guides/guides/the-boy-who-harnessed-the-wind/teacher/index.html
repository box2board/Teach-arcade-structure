Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does William turn knowledge into a practical proposal under severe constraints?

  • Explain how problem solving shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce energy conversion, prototype, famine, and access. Supply a teacher-selected scientific diagram and a memoir excerpt; the film is not a turbine-building safety tutorial. Discuss Malawi as a real country with varied experiences.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Famine, hunger, theft, violence, and a death in the family.
  • Threats, public beating, political repression, and intense family distress.
  • Some language and hardship involving children.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after school access is lost, after the library investigation, after the small model, and after the bicycle dispute.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The film dramatizes events and compresses technical and social processes. Trace the proposed use of wind for electricity and pumping, then check the mechanics against an appropriate source. Food crises have multiple environmental, economic, and political causes.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does William turn knowledge into a practical proposal under severe constraints?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What is the difference between an idea and a tested prototype?
    A prototype lets someone observe whether an idea works under particular conditions.
  2. How does William’s repair work establish his interests?
    It shows curiosity about devices and experience with practical problem solving.
  3. What pressures threaten the family’s harvest and food supply?
    Environmental problems and economic conditions combine to create severe scarcity.
  4. Why does William lose ordinary access to school?
    His family cannot meet the required payments.
  5. What connection does he make between wind and the community’s need?
    He proposes using wind-generated energy to help pump water for cultivation.
  6. How does studying an existing device support his thinking?
    It connects a mechanical example with the possibility of producing electricity.
  7. Why is a small working model valuable?
    It provides observable evidence for a larger proposal rather than relying on a promise alone.
  8. Why does his father resist giving up the bicycle?
    It is a valuable resource, and the larger plan seems uncertain during an emergency.
  9. What changes when others support the construction?
    Shared labor and materials help turn a private proposal into a community effort.
  10. What does the resolution emphasize beyond individual ingenuity?
    Access to knowledge, collective work, and practical support matter to the result.
  11. Which scene best demonstrates evidence changing a judgment?
    Describe an observation or demonstration and the response it produces.
  12. How would you verify the film’s technical explanation?
    Compare one depicted mechanism with a teacher-provided scientific source or diagram.
  13. How would a memoir excerpt help evaluate a scene?
    It can reveal differences in chronology, emphasis, or detail without making either medium exhaustive.

    Environmental problems and economic conditions combine to create severe scarcity.
  5. Why does William lose ordinary access to school?
    His family cannot meet the required payments.
  6. How does he continue learning after that barrier?
    He seeks access to the library and uses available texts and observations.
  7. What connection does he make between wind and the community’s need?
    He proposes using wind-generated energy to help pump water for cultivation.
  8. How does studying an existing device support his thinking?
    It connects a mechanical example with the possibility of producing electricity.
  9. Why is a small working model valuable?
    It provides observable evidence for a larger proposal rather than relying on a promise alone.
  10. How do political events affect the surrounding crisis?
    The film shows repression and limited responsiveness alongside the community’s needs.
  11. Why does his father resist giving up the bicycle?
    It is a valuable resource, and the larger plan seems uncertain during an emergency.
  12. How does the dispute reveal different kinds of knowledge?
    William’s technical learning confronts his father’s experience of risk and survival.
  13. What changes when others support the construction?
    Shared labor and materials help turn a private proposal into a community effort.
  14. Why should the finished device be analyzed as a system?
    Its parts must transfer energy and work together rather than function independently.
  15. How does the outcome change the family relationship?
    Evidence and cooperation help rebuild trust around William’s proposal.
  16. What does the resolution emphasize beyond individual ingenuity?
    Access to knowledge, collective work, and practical support matter to the result.
  17. Which scene best demonstrates evidence changing a judgment?
    Describe an observation or demonstration and the response it produces.
  18. What factor could a simple invention narrative understate?
    Identify a social or environmental condition that the device alone cannot resolve.
  19. How would you verify the film’s technical explanation?
    Compare one depicted mechanism with a teacher-provided scientific source or diagram.
  20. How would a memoir excerpt help evaluate a scene?
    It can reveal differences in chronology, emphasis, or detail without making either medium exhaustive.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
