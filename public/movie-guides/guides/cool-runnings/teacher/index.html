Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does the team learn to pursue success without abandoning its identity?

  • Explain how teamwork shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Introduce teamwork, integrity, stereotype, and dramatization. Explain that the characters and coach are substantially fictionalized. Use a teacher-selected Olympic source to compare one event; do not infer team history from comic scenes alone.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Sport-related crashes and physical danger.
  • Discriminatory comments, fights, insults, and some language.
  • Drinking and discussion of a coach’s cheating.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after team formation, after the first unfamiliar training, after the copied starting routine fails, and after the final run.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The real team’s path differs from the film’s sprinting premise and dramatic rivalries. Analyze the film’s message while identifying uncertainty about specific gestures, conversations, or opposition. A Jamaican identity is not a single shared personality.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Disney film information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does the team learn to pursue success without abandoning its identity?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes teamwork more than doing the same task together?
    Members coordinate roles, build trust, and respond to one another’s needs.
  2. Why does Derice seek a new Olympic route?
    A failed sprint qualification leads him to consider another way to compete.
  3. Why is a bobsled team an unfamiliar proposal in their setting?
    The winter sport requires conditions, equipment, and experience not readily available locally.
  4. How does Sanka contribute beyond fitting an athlete stereotype?
    His experience, humor, and willingness to join help the team develop.
  5. What does finding a coach make possible?
    Instruction and experience give the ambitious plan a more practical basis.
  6. How does raising money reveal community involvement?
    Support and persuasion help obtain resources the team cannot supply alone.
  7. What does arrival in Calgary make newly difficult?
    Cold, unfamiliar procedures, and public assumptions create additional demands.
  8. Why does copying another team’s routine fail to solve their problem?
    Imitation can ignore their own communication and identity.
  9. What does the coach’s past introduce into the story?
    A history of cheating creates a contrast between winning and trustworthy conduct.
  10. What does carrying the sled communicate within the film?
    It presents collective resolve and dignity beyond the official race outcome.
  11. Which scene shows a change in how the team cooperates?
    Explain a specific shift in communication or trust.
  12. Which historical claim would you check with an Olympic source?
    Select a scene or premise and distinguish inspiration from documentation.
  13. What does the ending leave out about long-term success?
    One race cannot show every later opportunity, cost, or institutional change.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes teamwork more than doing the same task together?
    Members coordinate roles, build trust, and respond to one another’s needs.
  2. How can a success goal conflict with integrity?
    Pressure to win can encourage shortcuts that violate rules or values.
  3. Why does Derice seek a new Olympic route?
    A failed sprint qualification leads him to consider another way to compete.
  4. Why is a bobsled team an unfamiliar proposal in their setting?
    The winter sport requires conditions, equipment, and experience not readily available locally.
  5. How does Sanka contribute beyond fitting an athlete stereotype?
    His experience, humor, and willingness to join help the team develop.
  6. Why do the members initially struggle to work together?
    Personal tensions and different expectations interfere with coordination.
  7. What does finding a coach make possible?
    Instruction and experience give the ambitious plan a more practical basis.
  8. How does raising money reveal community involvement?
    Support and persuasion help obtain resources the team cannot supply alone.
  9. What does arrival in Calgary make newly difficult?
    Cold, unfamiliar procedures, and public assumptions create additional demands.
  10. How does ridicule affect the team’s response?
    It adds pressure and raises questions about belonging without proving their ability.
  11. Why does copying another team’s routine fail to solve their problem?
    Imitation can ignore their own communication and identity.
  12. How does developing their own starting approach help?
    A shared routine supports coordination and confidence on their terms.
  13. What does the coach’s past introduce into the story?
    A history of cheating creates a contrast between winning and trustworthy conduct.
  14. Why does Junior’s relationship with his father matter?
    He must negotiate expected status and the activity he chooses with his teammates.
  15. What happens to their final competitive goal?
    The crash prevents the hoped-for result despite preparation and effort.
  16. What does carrying the sled communicate within the film?
    It presents collective resolve and dignity beyond the official race outcome.
  17. Which scene shows a change in how the team cooperates?
    Explain a specific shift in communication or trust.
  18. How does the film challenge and also risk using stereotypes?
    Identify a particular portrayal and explain its effect rather than generalizing about a country.
  19. Which historical claim would you check with an Olympic source?
    Select a scene or premise and distinguish inspiration from documentation.
  20. What does the ending leave out about long-term success?
    One race cannot show every later opportunity, cost, or institutional change.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
