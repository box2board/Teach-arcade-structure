Own the Room Teacher Guide

Plan the lesson and review the answer keys for both student formats.

Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: What makes a compelling pitch, and what does a competition leave out about a business?

  • Distinguish filmed observations and participant accounts from interpretation.
  • Support an analysis with two accurately described moments from this documentary.
  • Identify how selection, editing, or missing perspectives limit a conclusion.

Introduce customer need, value proposition, evidence, pitch, and selection. Ask students to analyze observed participants rather than disclose financial circumstances. Explain that a compelling business story is not proof of profitability or impact.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • News footage of violent unrest includes artillery and a dead body; hurricane destruction is shown.
  • Accounts of poverty, civil unrest, and airport detention and deportation.
  • Adult drinking, bleeped language, and mild sexual references; the studio lists TV-14.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on participants; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the participant introductions, after contrasting preparation scenes, before the final pitches, and after the outcome.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The filmmakers select five journeys and organize them around a prize competition. Consider how access to travel, education, networks, and funding affects opportunities. No participant should be treated as representing an entire country.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

Documentary studio information · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • What makes a compelling pitch, and what does a competition leave out about a business?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes a business proposal more than an appealing idea?
    It identifies a need and provides evidence about a workable response.
  2. Choose one participant and identify the problem their business addresses.
    Name the person and accurately describe the need shown in the film.
  3. What evidence does that participant offer about the proposed solution?
    Identify a demonstration, customer experience, results, or another concrete example.
  4. How does another participant’s proposal differ?
    Compare the actual need and response rather than ranking entire countries.
  5. How does the film connect a personal story with a business purpose?
    Use a particular introduction or interview to explain the connection.
  6. What obstacle complicates one participant’s preparation?
    Identify a documented difficulty and its effect on the project.
  7. How does the participant respond to that obstacle?
    Describe an observed action rather than assuming effort alone resolves it.
  8. How does access differ between two participants?
    Compare one specific condition such as equipment, funding, education, or networks.
  9. How does a participant revise or rehearse a pitch?
    Describe one preparation choice and the audience concern it addresses.
  10. What business question remains unanswered after the award?
    Identify a question about customers, cost, sustainability, or later impact.
  11. Compare two ways the film defines success.
    Support a contrast involving recognition, useful work, learning, or personal opportunity.
  12. What claim would you independently verify before supporting a proposal?
    Choose a concrete claim and an appropriate source or measurement.
  13. What limitation follows from the film’s selection of five competitors?
    Their edited journeys cannot represent every entrepreneur or every route to success.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What makes a business proposal more than an appealing idea?
    It identifies a need and provides evidence about a workable response.
  2. Why is winning a pitch contest not the same as long-term success?
    Judging occurs at one moment and cannot establish every later business outcome.
  3. Choose one participant and identify the problem their business addresses.
    Name the person and accurately describe the need shown in the film.
  4. What evidence does that participant offer about the proposed solution?
    Identify a demonstration, customer experience, results, or another concrete example.
  5. How does another participant’s proposal differ?
    Compare the actual need and response rather than ranking entire countries.
  6. What does the home context add to a participant’s story?
    Describe a specific resource, responsibility, or experience that affects preparation.
  7. How does the film connect a personal story with a business purpose?
    Use a particular introduction or interview to explain the connection.
  8. What obstacle complicates one participant’s preparation?
    Identify a documented difficulty and its effect on the project.
  9. How does the participant respond to that obstacle?
    Describe an observed action rather than assuming effort alone resolves it.
  10. Where does support become visible?
    Identify a mentor, family member, institution, or resource that contributes.
  11. How does access differ between two participants?
    Compare one specific condition such as equipment, funding, education, or networks.
  12. What does travel to the competition change?
    It creates shared pressure and contact with other proposals beyond home contexts.
  13. How does a participant revise or rehearse a pitch?
    Describe one preparation choice and the audience concern it addresses.
  14. What makes one final pitch persuasive?
    Identify a clear claim and supporting evidence, distinguishing emotion from verification.
  15. How does a participant respond to the competition result?
    Use observed behavior or statements without inventing private feelings.
  16. What business question remains unanswered after the award?
    Identify a question about customers, cost, sustainability, or later impact.
  17. Compare two ways the film defines success.
    Support a contrast involving recognition, useful work, learning, or personal opportunity.
  18. How does editing shape comparison among participants?
    Explain how alternating stories or contrasting settings affects interpretation.
  19. What claim would you independently verify before supporting a proposal?
    Choose a concrete claim and an appropriate source or measurement.
  20. What limitation follows from the film’s selection of five competitors?
    Their edited journeys cannot represent every entrepreneur or every route to success.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
