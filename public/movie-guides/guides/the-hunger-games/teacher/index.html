Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How does the Capitol use spectacle to control people, and where does that control fail?

  • Explain how power & spectacle shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with a selected passage from its source or another relevant text.

Introduce propaganda, spectacle, audience, and dystopia. Preview the violence involving children. Use the first 2012 film and provide a selected Collins passage; do not turn the lethal competition into a classroom simulation.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Children and teenagers are forced to fight to the death; killings, blood, and injury.
  • A young child’s death, oppressive threats, and intense pursuit.
  • Some language, alcohol use, and scenes of deprivation.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the reaping, after the interviews, after Rue’s death, and before the final rule reversal.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The invented society invites political and media analysis without establishing a direct equivalence to any one real country. Separate Katniss’s intentions from the meanings audiences and officials give her actions.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

AFI film credits · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How does the Capitol use spectacle to control people, and where does that control fail?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can entertainment serve political power?
    A spectacle can distract, intimidate, or shape beliefs while appearing to offer enjoyment.
  2. Why does Katniss volunteer at the reaping?
    She acts to protect Prim from being sent to the Games.
  3. What does the contrast between District 12 and the Capitol show?
    Differences in wealth and consumption make unequal power visible.
  4. How do stylists and interviews shape the tributes’ public identities?
    They package individuals into appealing images for viewers and sponsors.
  5. How does Haymitch connect audience response with survival?
    He explains that gaining attention and sponsors can provide practical assistance.
  6. How does the arena demonstrate that the competition is controlled?
    Officials manipulate conditions and threats rather than merely observe a fair contest.
  7. Why does Katniss form an alliance with Rue?
    Trust and complementary skills support survival across district boundaries.
  8. How does Katniss respond to Rue’s death?
    Her care and tribute reject treating Rue as disposable entertainment.
  9. How does the announced rule change alter Katniss’s plan?
    The possibility of two winners from one district gives her reason to seek Peeta.
  10. What does the threatened use of the berries accomplish?
    It challenges the Capitol’s need for a victor and forces officials to restore the joint outcome.
  11. Which scene most clearly reveals propaganda or media framing?
    Describe a specific presentation and explain whose interests it serves.
  12. How does a selected novel passage change access to Katniss’s thoughts?
    Compare first-person narration with performance and the film’s additional viewpoints.
  13. What conflict remains after the apparent victory?
    The Capitol still controls the system and reads the outcome as a potential challenge.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. How can entertainment serve political power?
    A spectacle can distract, intimidate, or shape beliefs while appearing to offer enjoyment.
  2. Why can different audiences interpret the same act differently?
    Their knowledge, interests, and expectations affect its meaning.
  3. Why does Katniss volunteer at the reaping?
    She acts to protect Prim from being sent to the Games.
  4. What does the contrast between District 12 and the Capitol show?
    Differences in wealth and consumption make unequal power visible.
  5. How do stylists and interviews shape the tributes’ public identities?
    They package individuals into appealing images for viewers and sponsors.
  6. Why does Peeta’s interview statement matter?
    It changes how the audience understands their relationship and their possible appeal.
  7. How does Haymitch connect audience response with survival?
    He explains that gaining attention and sponsors can provide practical assistance.
  8. How does the arena demonstrate that the competition is controlled?
    Officials manipulate conditions and threats rather than merely observe a fair contest.
  9. Why does Katniss form an alliance with Rue?
    Trust and complementary skills support survival across district boundaries.
  10. What does their attack on the supplies accomplish?
    It challenges the advantages held by the stronger alliance.
  11. How does Katniss respond to Rue’s death?
    Her care and tribute reject treating Rue as disposable entertainment.
  12. Why can that response have political meaning?
    It shows shared humanity across divisions the Capitol uses to maintain control.
  13. How does the announced rule change alter Katniss’s plan?
    The possibility of two winners from one district gives her reason to seek Peeta.
  14. How do private feelings and performance become difficult to separate?
    Their relationship is watched and rewarded, making motives complex for characters and viewers.
  15. Why does the last rule reversal expose unequal power?
    Officials can change the terms after the tributes have acted on a previous promise.
  16. What does the threatened use of the berries accomplish?
    It challenges the Capitol’s need for a victor and forces officials to restore the joint outcome.
  17. Which scene most clearly reveals propaganda or media framing?
    Describe a specific presentation and explain whose interests it serves.
  18. How does the film make viewers reflect on watching violence?
    Analyze a specific camera, editing, or audience scene without treating suffering as a game.
  19. How does a selected novel passage change access to Katniss’s thoughts?
    Compare first-person narration with performance and the film’s additional viewpoints.
  20. What conflict remains after the apparent victory?
    The Capitol still controls the system and reads the outcome as a potential challenge.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
