Teaching Notes

Learning goals & preparation

Essential question: How can a community reconsider a rule while taking its fears seriously?

  • Explain how community rules shapes character choices.
  • Support an interpretation with two accurately described scenes.
  • Compare the portrayal with an appropriate external source and identify what remains uncertain.

Use the 1984 film, not the 2011 remake. Introduce policy, claim, evidence, and compromise. Despite the PG rating, preview the mature language, sexual references, and dangerous behavior for your class.

Content considerations & viewing plan
  • Language, sexual references, implied teen sex, brief rear nudity, drinking, smoking, and drug references.
  • Abusive boyfriend behavior, fights, and dangerous stunts with vehicles and trains.
  • A fatal past crash and grief shape the conflict; religious disagreements require respectful discussion.

Preview the assigned edition and arrange required permissions and an alternative source-based assignment. Students can explain harm without describing graphic acts or repeating slurs. Keep discussion focused on characters; do not require personal disclosures.

Pause after the dance ban is explained, after Ren’s council argument, after the book-burning confrontation, and before the dance.

Use brief responses during viewing and reserve interpretation for afterward. Check subtitles and captions. If scenes are omitted, bridge the narrative and adapt the affected questions.

Context & source comparison

The fictional town simplifies a broader debate about expression and control. Examine Ren’s case and the adults’ concerns without assuming that defiance makes every action wise. Avoid asking students to disclose family or religious conflict.

For a short comparison, select one accessible source or excerpt, establish author/date/purpose, and compare one film claim with what it supports and leaves unanswered. Teacher selection and adaptation are required.

AFI credits and production history · Content advisory (spoilers)

Discussion & optional extensions
  • How can a community reconsider a rule while taking its fears seriously?
  • Which scene best supports your interpretation, and what might another viewer notice?
  • What does the film leave out or simplify?

Exit ticket: One claim, one supporting scene, and one question for further study.

Optional extension: Build an evidence chart with columns for film portrayal, external source, agreement or difference, and remaining uncertainty. Assess accuracy and reasoning.

Paper Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 13 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What should a community consider when judging a rule?
    Its purpose, evidence, effects, fairness, and whether a less restrictive alternative exists.
  2. Why does Ren feel out of place in Bomont?
    He is a newcomer whose interests and behavior conflict with local expectations.
  3. What past event helps explain the dance ban?
    A fatal crash involving local young people contributes to fear and the response of authorities.
  4. How does Reverend Moore connect authority with protection?
    He treats restrictive rules as a way to prevent further harm and preserve order.
  5. Why should the film’s dangerous stunts be questioned?
    Risk-taking can harm people even when a character feels misunderstood or wants independence.
  6. How does Ren’s friendship with Willard change his experience?
    Practical help, humor, and shared effort provide support within the unfamiliar town.
  7. What does teaching Willard to dance show about learning?
    Practice and encouragement allow a skill to develop without treating embarrassment as failure.
  8. Why does he use biblical passages in his argument?
    He appeals to a source respected by his audience to challenge its interpretation of dancing.
  9. How does the book-burning incident challenge Reverend Moore?
    It makes the danger of excessive control visible and leads him to oppose that action.
  10. What does the final dance communicate through music and movement?
    It presents shared expression and participation as a moment of community connection.
  11. Which action offers the strongest model of constructive disagreement?
    Accept a supported example involving preparation, listening, dialogue, or a practical alternative.
  12. How does music shape our response to one scene?
    Explain a specific connection among sound, action, and the viewer’s interpretation.
  13. What does the ending leave unresolved about the community?
    A celebration does not establish how future disagreements or safety concerns will be handled.
Online Worksheet Answer Key

Expected answers for 20 questions. Accept supported alternatives for analytical prompts.

  1. What should a community consider when judging a rule?
    Its purpose, evidence, effects, fairness, and whether a less restrictive alternative exists.
  2. What makes persuasion different from merely demanding agreement?
    It addresses reasons and concerns rather than relying only on pressure or insult.
  3. Why does Ren feel out of place in Bomont?
    He is a newcomer whose interests and behavior conflict with local expectations.
  4. What past event helps explain the dance ban?
    A fatal crash involving local young people contributes to fear and the response of authorities.
  5. How does Reverend Moore connect authority with protection?
    He treats restrictive rules as a way to prevent further harm and preserve order.
  6. How does Ariel’s relationship with her father reveal tension?
    Her actions and their conversations expose a gap between control and understanding.
  7. Why should the film’s dangerous stunts be questioned?
    Risk-taking can harm people even when a character feels misunderstood or wants independence.
  8. How does Ren’s friendship with Willard change his experience?
    Practical help, humor, and shared effort provide support within the unfamiliar town.
  9. What does teaching Willard to dance show about learning?
    Practice and encouragement allow a skill to develop without treating embarrassment as failure.
  10. How does Ren prepare a case for changing the rule?
    He gathers support and organizes reasons that address the community’s own values.
  11. Why does he use biblical passages in his argument?
    He appeals to a source respected by his audience to challenge its interpretation of dancing.
  12. Does the council immediately accept his argument?
    No; the vote shows that a reasoned appeal does not guarantee an immediate policy change.
  13. How does the book-burning incident challenge Reverend Moore?
    It makes the danger of excessive control visible and leads him to oppose that action.
  14. How do private conversations influence reconsideration?
    Listening and acknowledgment of grief help change positions beyond the formal vote.
  15. Why is the dance organized outside the town’s restriction?
    The location provides a practical way forward while the broader conflict is reconsidered.
  16. What does the final dance communicate through music and movement?
    It presents shared expression and participation as a moment of community connection.
  17. Which action offers the strongest model of constructive disagreement?
    Accept a supported example involving preparation, listening, dialogue, or a practical alternative.
  18. How could a revised policy address both expression and safety?
    Propose a supported alternative such as supervised events and clear conduct expectations.
  19. How does music shape our response to one scene?
    Explain a specific connection among sound, action, and the viewer’s interpretation.
  20. What does the ending leave unresolved about the community?
    A celebration does not establish how future disagreements or safety concerns will be handled.

Final writing: Look for a clear claim, two accurately described scenes, reasoning, and a meaningful limitation of the portrayal or a question for further study. Accept different supported interpretations. No graphic descriptions are required.
