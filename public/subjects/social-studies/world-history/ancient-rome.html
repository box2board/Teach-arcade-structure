Ancient Rome

Concise, classroom-ready guidance for the Roman Republic, Empire, society, achievements, and transformation.

Teaching Ancient Rome

Use these points to frame instruction, discussion, or a quick debrief.

Republic and government

Rome’s republic divided power among elected officials, assemblies, and the Senate, though participation and influence were unequal.

Society and citizenship

Patricians, plebeians, enslaved people, women, and noncitizens experienced different rights and roles.

Expansion and empire

Military organization, alliances, roads, and conquest expanded Roman control while creating political and economic pressures.

Law and engineering

Roman legal traditions and projects such as roads, aqueducts, and concrete construction shaped the empire and later societies.

Religion and Christianity

Roman religion absorbed traditions from across the empire; Christianity grew from a persecuted movement into an officially supported faith.

Western empire’s decline

Political instability, economic strain, military pressure, and division weakened western rule; the eastern empire continued for centuries.

Try This in Class

Give groups one Roman institution or achievement—such as the Senate, citizenship, roads, or law—and ask them to trace one benefit, one limitation, and one lasting influence.
