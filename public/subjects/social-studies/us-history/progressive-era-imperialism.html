Teaching the Progressive Era

Use these compact points to frame the game, clarify vocabulary, or guide a short debrief.

Problems of industrial growth

Rapid industrialization and urbanization intensified concerns about unsafe work, concentrated corporate power, poverty, and government corruption.

Investigators and reformers

Muckrakers and settlement-house leaders publicized conditions and organized responses, turning evidence into pressure for change.

Political and social reform

Direct democracy measures, direct election of senators, and women’s suffrage sought to expand public influence in government.

Regulation and protection

Antitrust efforts, food and drug laws, and labor reforms expanded expectations that government should protect workers and consumers.

Reform and U.S. power

Study domestic reform alongside imperialism to compare debates over democracy and power at home and abroad.

Try This in Class
