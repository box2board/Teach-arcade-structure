Subject Hubs Built for Teacher Planning

Teach Arcade subject hubs are designed to reduce planning time while increasing classroom engagement. Instead of searching across dozens of disconnected websites, you can begin with one hub, choose your grade band, and move straight into ready-to-use lessons, review games, and skill practice.

Each hub is written for classroom implementation. You will find topic groupings, practical activity ideas, and instructional pathways for elementary, middle, and high school. Whether you are prepping tomorrow's warm-up or mapping next month's unit, these pages help you make decisions quickly and teach with confidence.

How teachers use these hubs in real classrooms

Most teachers use this page in one of three ways. First, they start with a subject and pull a fast bell-ringer, station, or review game for immediate use. Second, they use the hub as a weekly planning map, selecting one skill focus and one practice format for each day. Third, departments and PLC teams use these hub pages to align instruction across grade levels by looking at skill progression and vocabulary emphasis.

Example workflow: A grade 7 team planning proportional reasoning can open Math, select middle school, pull a short concept refresher, assign a collaborative game-based practice activity, and close with an exit ticket reflection prompt. The same structure works in ELA for claim-evidence writing, in Science for modeling investigations, and in Social Studies for source analysis.

When to use this resource: quick decision aid

  • Use subject hubs when you need speed: You want to teach a strong lesson without spending hours searching.
  • Use subject hubs when students need engagement: You need interactive review or structured practice before an assessment.
  • Use subject hubs when differentiating: You want topic pathways that scale from foundational to extension activities.
  • Use subject hubs when collaborating: You want a shared planning reference for grade-level or department teams.

Grade-band guidance

Elementary: Prioritize routine, concrete examples, and short practice cycles. Use visual supports and frequent checks for understanding.

Middle school: Focus on concept transfer, discussion protocols, and low-stakes retrieval. Rotate between direct instruction and collaborative challenge tasks.

High school: Emphasize rigor, evidence-based reasoning, and independent application. Pair review games with analysis tasks and writing-to-learn prompts.
