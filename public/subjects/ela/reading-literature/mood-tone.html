Teaching mood and tone

Use these points to frame instruction, discussion, or a quick debrief.

Tone is the author’s attitude

Tone describes the author’s or speaker’s attitude toward a subject, revealed through diction, details, and style.

Mood is the reader’s response

Mood is the emotional atmosphere a text creates for the reader.

Word choice matters

Connotation, imagery, and specific details can shift tone or create a different mood.

Evidence anchors interpretation

Students should name a precise word, image, or detail and explain how it supports their tone or mood claim.

Try This in Class

When a question orb appears, require an evidence sentence before selecting an answer: “The word or phrase ___ suggests ___ because…” Then replace one key word and discuss what changes.
