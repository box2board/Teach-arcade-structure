DESIGN CHALLENGE / CURRENT, RESISTANCE & POWER

Build a reliable warning circuit.

A model circuit must operate within two limits. Can your evidence justify a design?

Start with the design brief.

01 / THE PROBLEM

A signal that can be trusted.

A school is evaluating a battery-powered warning module. Your team is testing its electrical supply with a resistor as a dummy load. Too little current could fail to operate the module; too much power would exceed this test load’s rating.

Your job: recommend a supply voltage and nominal load resistance, supported by repeated measurements and calculations.

OPERATING CURRENT35–55 mA
LOAD POWER LIMIT≤ 0.250 W

These are specifications for this fictional test module. The resistor represents a test load, not a real LED, buzzer, or complete warning device.

What you will learn

  • Design a controlled comparison and use repeat measurements.
  • Measure current in series and voltage in parallel.
  • Use I = V/R and P = VI to analyze a design.
  • Evaluate measurement variation, component tolerance, and evidence limits.
THE DESIGN ENVELOPE

Available parts

1–12 V DC supply settings, in 1 V steps.
40–200 Ω nominal resistance, in 10 Ω steps.
Ammeter, voltmeter, switch, and leads.

Important limits

We are studying a low-voltage virtual circuit. A real device would also need environmental testing, protection, a suitable power source, and component-specific checks.

Your deliverable

A lab report with a test plan, raw data, calculations, a controlled-comparison graph, and a claim supported by evidence.

    03 / ASSEMBLE & CHECK

    Drag a tool onto its circuit position. Or select, then tap a target.

    Switch open · power isolated
    No readings taken.

    A = ammeter in series; V = voltmeter across the load. Readings are revealed only after you sample both instruments. Record them with units.

    LAB PROCEDURE
    1. With the switch open, assemble the supply, load, leads, switch, and both meters.
    2. Zero-check the ammeter with no current flowing.
    3. Choose a setting from your plan. Keep the control constant.
    4. Close the switch. Sample both instruments and record their readings.
    5. Open the switch, then repeat. Collect three readings at each of at least three settings.
    6. Use the analysis tab to calculate and graph. Test a candidate design three times before recommending it.

    04 / ANALYZE YOUR EVIDENCE

    Calculate before you recommend.

    Calculation support

    Mean current = sum of measured currents ÷ number of trials.
    Predicted current (mA) = supply setting (V) ÷ nominal resistance (Ω) × 1000.
    Load power (W) = mean measured voltage (V) × mean current (mA) ÷ 1000.
    Percent difference = |mean measured current − predicted current| ÷ predicted current × 100.

    Report mean and predicted current to 0.1 mA, power to 0.001 W, and percent difference to 0.1%. The denominator uses the ideal prediction, so this is a percent deviation from that prediction.
