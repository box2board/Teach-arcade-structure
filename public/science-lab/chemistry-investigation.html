What does the neutral point tell us?

In this 1:1 reaction, acid concentration × acid volume = base concentration × base volume at equivalence. The pH changes sharply near this point. You may pass from below pH 7 to above pH 7 without measuring exactly 7.

We estimate the endpoint halfway between the last acidic reading and first basic reading. That interval is your evidence, not an exact reading of neutrality. After 10.0 mL, the dose decreases from 5.0 mL to 0.2 mL to narrow the interval.

Each repeat uses a fresh nominal 20.0 mL sample. Tiny modeled sample-volume and delivery variation, plus pH measurement noise, may shift the estimated endpoint. Repeated results may still agree at the displayed resolution.
