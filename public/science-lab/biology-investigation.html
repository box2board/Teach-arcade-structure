Why can a solution change the mass of plant tissue?

Test similar-sized potato cylinders in 0.0, 0.3, and 0.6 M sucrose. Weigh each one before and after a modeled 30-minute soak.

Use three fresh cylinders and fresh solution aliquots per concentration. Keep sample preparation, solution volume, temperature, soak time, and blotting method the same. Only solution concentration changes.

Why blot the sample? Why use percentage change?

Liquid clinging to the outside would add mass that did not enter the tissue. Blot gently and consistently before the final weighing. Squeezing the cylinder would introduce another error.

Percentage mass change compares samples with slightly different starting masses: (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100. We calculate it automatically; you can try a calculation yourself at the end.

Water moves in both directions across selectively permeable cell membranes. Our measurements indicate the net effect on tissue mass. Near-zero change is consistent with little net water movement under these conditions; it does not mean that water molecules stopped moving.
